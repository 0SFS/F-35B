# F-35B air-to-surface stores

Public, unclassified inventory as of 22 October 2026. Scope is expendable air-to-surface weapons and external fuel tanks that compete with weapon stations. Guidance is recorded only as a class. Warhead fill, fuze design, seeker design, and route-planning procedures are omitted even where a brochure prints them. A number that was not in a retrieved source is written as not published. Dates are the date on the document, or “retrieved 22 October 2026” when the page itself is undated.

Station map used below: the F-35 family has four internal weapon stations and seven external stations. The internal bays combine an air-to-ground station and an air-to-air station in each bay. — [F-35 Joint Program Office briefing, RADM Mahr, 17 March 2015](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2015/PSAR/RADMMahr.pdf)

## Which stores fit an F-35B bay, one or two per bay, or external stations only, and is the store cleared on the B?

### Takeaway

The published F-35B internal air-to-ground bay is a 1,000 lb-class bay, one store per bay on the legacy air-to-ground station: the standard internal bomb is the GBU-32, and the 2,000 lb GBU-31 is the A and C internal store. UK Paveway IV is carried both internally and externally on the F-35 and is an in-service UK F-35B weapon. SPEAR 3 has been captive-carried, four at a time, and is planned at eight per F-35B, but it has not been released. JSM and AARGM-ER internal carriage is published for the A and C, not the B; AARGM-ER external carriage is claimed for all three variants. No retrieved program document confirms an eight-SDB internal load specifically inside the B bays.

### Cited Findings

**Bay class and station ratings**

- Internal weapon bays are “2000 lb on F-35 A and F-35C” and “1000 lb on F-35B,” with internal adapters. The same 17 March 2015 briefing lists six external air-to-air and air-to-ground hardpoints, a centerline station on all versions that is structurally tailored on the F-35B, an internal GAU-22 on the A, and a missionized gun on the B and C. — [F-35 JPO, 17 March 2015](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2015/PSAR/RADMMahr.pdf)
- Lockheed Martin’s variant material gives the F-35B a standard internal weapons load of two AIM-120C/D and two 1,000-pound GBU-32 JDAMs, a weapons payload of 15,000 lb / 6,800 kg, and a maximum weight in the 60,000 lb class. The same material gives the F-35A and F-35C a standard internal load of two AIM-120C/D and two 2,000-pound GBU-31 JDAMs, and an 18,000 lb-class weapons payload. — [Lockheed Martin F-35 variant presentation](https://view.ceros.com/lockheed-martin/f-35-variants), undated, retrieved 22 October 2026
- A 2012 F-35 program briefing lists the Block 2B STOVL internal set as “2 GBU-12, 2 GBU-32, 2 AMRAAM,” marked “All Internal Only,” and the CTOL and CV internal set as “2 GBU-12, 2 GBU-31, 2 AMRAAM.” The F-35B Block 3 chart in that briefing also names GBU-31 (Mk 84 and BLU-109), AGM-154A/C, GBU-32, GBU-12, Paveway IV, AIM-9X, a missionized gun, and AIM-132 ASRAAM. The text extract does not cleanly label which of the Block 3 B stores sit in the external columns versus the internal column. — [F-35 program status and weapons roadmap, NDIA, 2012](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2012/annual_psr/WERTH.pdf)
- The same 2012 chart’s text extract rates STOVL outboard positions at 1,500 lb where the CTOL/CV chart shows 2,500 lb, inboard positions at 5,000 lb, wingtips at 300 lb, internal air-to-air stations at 350 lb, and the centerline at 1,000 lb. The extract also shows 2,500 lb on positions consistent with the internal air-to-ground stations, including the STOVL row. Column alignment in the text extract is imperfect. — [NDIA, 2012](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2012/annual_psr/WERTH.pdf)

**Paveway II and UK Paveway IV**

- On 12 June 2015, RAF test pilot Andy Edgell released two inert 500-pound Paveway IV bombs from F-35B BF-03. The Pax River Integrated Test Force described safe separation “from an internal weapons bay” and called the event part of Block 3F testing of the UK-unique Paveway IV. The release calls Paveway IV a dual-mode bomb and “the first dual mode bomb operational on the F-35,” which is the test team’s wording at first separation, not a later fleet-IOC notice. — [F-35 Pax River ITF via DVIDS, story date 12 June 2015, posted 18 June 2015](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/167123/f-35b-lightning-ii-aces-first-release-uk-paveway-iv-bomb)
- The current RAF Lightning F-35B page lists Paveway IV as a weapons system, with Meteor, AMRAAM, and ASRAAM. — [Royal Air Force, Lightning F-35B](https://www.raf.mod.uk/aircraft/current-aircraft/lightning-f35b/), undated, retrieved 22 October 2026
- UK MOD Form 706D(PWIV)(F-35), established April 2025, is the Paveway IV preparation and air-carriage record for the F-35. The form’s weapon-type block has Live and Practice, Standard and “Tac Pen,” and Internal and External. That is a primary statement that Paveway IV is prepared for both internal and external carriage on the F-35. The form does not say how many rounds fit in one bay. — [UK MOD Form 706D(PWIV)(F-35), established April 2025](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/680b6a73faff81833fcae8c6/706D_PWIV__F-35_.pdf); instructions [MOD Form 799](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/680b6d1956bc2cfe7f7f5b8e/799_PWIV__F-35_.pdf)
- GBU-12 is the 500 lb Paveway II named in the 2012 STOVL internal set (two GBU-12 in that set). — [NDIA, 2012](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2012/annual_psr/WERTH.pdf). An Air Force caption describes the GBU-12 as a 500 lb laser-guided bomb. — [U.S. Air Force art caption, GBU-12](https://www.af.mil/News/Art/igtag/bombs/), undated, retrieved 22 October 2026
- GBU-49 (Enhanced Paveway II) does not appear in the 2012 SDD charts or the 2015 JPO growth list retrieved here. A Lockheed-distributed infographic’s text extract mentions “2 AIM-120 & 2 GBU-49” among internal options, but the extract scrambles A, B, and C columns, so it is not used as a B-bay claim. — [F-35 infographic PDF](https://cdn2.hubspot.net/hubfs/2097098/F-35_infographic_R8.pdf), undated, retrieved 22 October 2026

### Inferences

- “Two GBU-32” and “two GBU-12” in the internal set are one air-to-ground store per bay, not two bombs in one bay. Each bay has one air-to-ground station. — from the 2015 station description and the Lockheed standard load of two GBU-32s on the B
- The 2012 phrase “2 GBU-12, 2 GBU-32, 2 AMRAAM” is a cleared-type list, not one simultaneous load of four bombs plus two missiles. Four air-to-ground bombs would exceed two air-to-ground stations.
- A 2,000 lb GBU-31 does not fit the published F-35B internal bay. If it is carried on a B at all, the bay-class statement puts it on an external station. The 2012 chart names it on the B plan but does not, in the text extract, prove that external clearance was finished.
- JSM internal carriage is an A/C feature. The brochure’s “external stations on F-35” is not a B clearance.
- AARGM-ER on an F-35B is external-only, on the manufacturer’s 2023 compatibility statement, and that statement is not a fleet clearance.
- Four SPEAR 3s in one bay, eight on the jet, follows from MBDA’s singular “payload bay,” the JPO’s “up to eight,” and two bays. Janes states the one-bay loading directly.
- A loaded BRU-61 at 1,397 lb and 143 inches sits above the 2015 “1000 lb” B-bay wording and in the length band of a 2,000 lb JDAM. Boeing still says the SDB carriage fits an F-35 internal bay, without saying which variant. Eight SDB Is or IIs inside a B should not be treated as published fact.
- JSOW at 160 inches is longer than a GBU-31. NAVAIR naming the F-35B as an AGM-154A/A-1/C platform does not say the store fits the B bay. The 2015 line “JSOW External” is the only carriage-location hint, and it is a growth label, not a clearance.
- External tanks, if ever cleared, would occupy the plumbed inboard wing stations and displace weapons there. No tank is cleared. A 426-gallon capacity was not in the sources above.

## What mass, length, and diameter are published?

### Takeaway

Service and manufacturer sheets publish length, weight, and a lateral dimension for the JDAM family, GBU-39, GBU-53, JSOW, and JSM. SPEAR 3 is published only as upper bounds. AARGM-ER, LRASM, JASSM, GBU-49, and the external tank have no published drawing dimensions in the sources retrieved. Paveway IV is published as a 500 lb / 225 kg class weapon; a primary length and diameter were not retrieved. Body diameter is often missing where wingspan is given, and two official GBU-39 widths disagree.

The sheet’s F-35 clearance sentence is stale and is not reused. — [U.S. Air Force JDAM fact sheet](https://www.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/104572/joint-direct-attack-munition-gbu-313238/)

## What guidance class is published?

### Takeaway

Published classes are GPS/INS for the JDAM family and SDB I; laser for GBU-12; GPS/INS plus laser for Paveway IV; GPS/INS plus millimeter-wave radar, laser, and infrared, with a data link, for SDB II; GPS/INS for JSOW, plus terminal imaging infrared on the C and C-1; GPS/INS plus terrain reference and imaging infrared for JSM. SPEAR 3 and AARGM-ER are published only as multi-sensor or multi-mode. No source retrieved here gives a guidance class for an unguided F-35 store, because no such store is listed.

### Cited Findings

- JDAM, including GBU-31, GBU-32, and GBU-38: GPS-aided INS. The fact sheet also discusses a possible future precision seeker; that is not the fielded JDAM class on this sheet. — [U.S. Air Force JDAM fact sheet](https://www.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/104572/joint-direct-attack-munition-gbu-313238/)
- GBU-12 Paveway II: laser-guided. The caption does not add GPS. — [U.S. Air Force art caption](https://www.af.mil/News/Art/igtag/bombs/)
- Paveway IV: inertial navigation and GPS, and the Pax River release calls it dual-mode. The release text emphasizes INS and GPS and does not spell the second mode in the sentences retrieved. Think Defence’s secondary page states semi-active laser as the second mode. — [DVIDS, 12 June 2015](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/167123/f-35b-lightning-ii-aces-first-release-uk-paveway-iv-bomb); [Think Defence](https://www.thinkdefence.co.uk/docs/paveway-iv/)
- GBU-39/B: INS/GPS, with anti-jam GPS. — [Boeing, 2015](https://www.boeing.com/content/dam/boeing/v2/products/precision-guided-munitions/documents/small_diameter_bomb_product_card.pdf). The Air Force fact sheet says GPS/INS only. — [U.S. Air Force SDB fact sheet](https://www.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/104573/gbu-39b-small-diameter-bomb-weapon-system/)
- GBU-53/B: “millimeter wave active radar homing, semi-active laser guidance, infrared homing, GPS coupled inertial guidance and data-link.” — [Air Combat Command](https://www.acc.af.mil/News/Article/2871447/gunfighters-first-shots-with-new-smart-bomb-stormbreaker/)
- AGM-154A/A-1/C: GPS/INS, and “Terminal IR Seeker (AGM-154C unique).” NAVAIR describes the C seeker as an uncooled long-wave imaging infrared seeker. AGM-154C-1: Link 16 data link, GPS/INS, and terminal infrared. — [NAVAIR](https://www.navair.navy.mil/product/jsow)
- JSM: “Inertial Navigation, aided by GPS and TERCOM,” and an “Imaging Infra-Red” seeker. Kongsberg also states a two-way data link. Passive radio-frequency guidance is not stated. — [Kongsberg brochure](https://www.aerocontact.com/public/img/aviaexpo/produits/catalogues/92/Brochure-Joint-Strike-Missile.pdf); data link also in [Kongsberg, 11 June 2013](https://www.kongsberg.com/news/news-archive/2013/successful-fit-check-for-jsm/)
- SPEAR: “an advanced multi-sensor seeker,” for moving and maneuvering targets on land and at sea. The MBDA page does not name GPS/INS, laser, infrared, radar, or passive radio frequency. — [MBDA SPEAR](https://www.mbda-systems.com/product/spear/); same limitation in the [21 May 2026 JPO release](https://www.f35.com/f35/news-and-features/F35B_achieves_first_flight_with_the_UKs_precision_long_range_strike_missile.html)
- AARGM-ER: “Utilizes AARGM multi-mode guidance section” and reuses “existing AARGM sensors.” The 2023 sheet does not name passive radio frequency, GPS/INS, or radar. — [Northrop Grumman, 2023](https://cdn.northropgrumman.com/-/media/Project/Northrop-Grumman/ngc/what-we-do/advanced-weapons/AARGM-ER-DataSheet-001.pdf?rev=b1a72cb37e414d0da4ccf3756130b7e7&sc_lang=en)
- LRASM: Lockheed says “precision routing, guidance and stealth” and that it navigates “semi-autonomously.” No class (GPS/INS, infrared, or passive radio frequency) is named on that page. — [Lockheed Martin](https://www.lockheedmartin.com/en-us/products/long-range-anti-ship-missile.html)
- GBU-54 appears only as a name on the 2015 growth slide. Guidance class is not stated there. — [JPO, 17 March 2015](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2015/PSAR/RADMMahr.pdf)

### Inferences

- Paveway IV’s second mode is laser if the secondary RAF-oriented page is accepted. The primary 2015 test release establishes dual-mode and explicitly names INS and GPS, not the laser mode, in the text retrieved.
- SDB II’s published classes cover every class the question listed except passive radio frequency: GPS/INS, laser, infrared, plus millimeter-wave radar and a data link.
- JSM is not a passive-RF anti-ship missile in the Kongsberg brochure. Its published classes are GPS/INS, terrain reference, and imaging infrared.

### Gaps

- SPEAR 3 sensor classes: not published on the MBDA page or the JPO flight release. Not filled in from memory.
- AARGM-ER mode list: not restated on the 2023 sheet. Not filled in from the earlier AGM-88E.
- LRASM and JASSM guidance class on a page that also ties the weapon to the F-35: not retrieved.
- GBU-49 and GBU-54 guidance class: not retrieved from a page opened for this note.
- Whether Paveway IV’s dual mode includes laser on the F-35 OFP, as opposed to the weapon’s general design: the primary test article does not say “laser.”

## Is any store prohibited in STOVL, or by the weapons-bay doors or lift-fan doors?

### Takeaway

No public source retrieved here prohibits carriage or release of these weapons in STOVL, or says the lift-fan doors block a store. The UK’s April 2025 Paveway IV form does the opposite of a ban: it contemplates STOVL takeoffs and landings with the weapon loaded, and it life-limits those events and weapons-bay door cycles. The numeric life limits sit in a register that was not retrieved.

### Cited Findings

- MOD Form 706D(PWIV)(F-35), established April 2025, has columns for STOVL takeoffs and STOVL landings, and for events remaining that include air-carriage hours, inboard door cycles, outboard door cycles, and STOVL. The instructions say those figures come from the component with the highest air-carriage life, the highest door cycles, or the highest STOVL takeoffs or landings, and that “The JSMCR states the lifing limitation for each data point.” JSMCR is the Joint Services Munitions Constraint Register. The form also records outboard door cycles at de-prep. Internal versus external is a carriage choice on the same form, so internal carriage is an expected state, not a prohibited one. — [MOD Form 706D, established April 2025](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/680b6a73faff81833fcae8c6/706D_PWIV__F-35_.pdf); [MOD Form 799 instructions](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/680b6d1956bc2cfe7f7f5b8e/799_PWIV__F-35_.pdf)
- The numeric STOVL count and the numeric door-cycle count are not printed on the form or in the instructions retrieved. They are “stated” to be in the JSMCR.
- Lockheed’s F-35B rollout description separates lift-fan doors, a dorsal auxiliary inlet, three-bearing-swivel-nozzle doors, and wing roll ducts from the weapons bays. It does not mention a weapons-bay restriction in STOVL. — [Lockheed Martin, F-35B unveiling release](https://investors.lockheedmartin.com/news-releases/news-release-details/first-short-takeoffvertical-landing-stealth-fighter-unveiled/)
- A Lockheed propulsion paper describes the F-35B lift fan, roll posts, auxiliary inlet, and three-bearing swivel module. The retrieved text does not prohibit a weapon, a bay door, or a release in STOVL. — [Lockheed Martin, F-35 Air Vehicle Technology Overview](https://www.lockheedmartin.com/content/dam/lockheed-martin/eo/documents/webt/F-35_Air_Vehicle_Technology_Overview.pdf)
- The 2012 and 2015 F-35 weapons briefings discuss STOVL station capacity and the 1,000 lb B bay. They do not prohibit any named store in STOVL mode or with the lift fan operating. — [NDIA, 2012](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2012/annual_psr/WERTH.pdf); [JPO, 17 March 2015](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2015/PSAR/RADMMahr.pdf)
- Public discussion notes that F-35B weapons-bay doors are sometimes partly open in hover. The explanations in that thread are speculative and the authors say they do not have an authoritative source. It is not a prohibition, and it is not used as a flight-manual statement. — [Aviation Stack Exchange](https://aviation.stackexchange.com/questions/94242/why-do-f-35b-jets-hover-with-their-weapons-bay-doors-partially-open)

### Inferences

- For Paveway IV, STOVL flight with the store aboard is an allowed, life-counted condition, and opening the weapons-bay doors is a life-counted condition. A simulator can treat both as normal carriage states. It cannot treat them as unlimited, and it cannot invent the JSMCR numbers.
- Nothing retrieved extends that life-limit form to JDAM, SDB, JSOW, JSM, SPEAR, AARGM-ER, or LRASM. Absence of a form is not evidence those stores are unrestricted, and it is not evidence they are banned.
- The lift fan has its own doors. No source says those doors share a mechanism with the weapons-bay doors or that they physically block an internal store.

### Gaps

- Joint Services Munitions Constraint Register values for Paveway IV STOVL takeoffs, STOVL landings, and inboard and outboard door cycles: not retrieved. The April 2025 form says the register holds them.
- A flight manual, NATOPS, or JPO statement that weapons-bay doors must be closed before conversion to STOVL, or that release is inhibited with the lift fan engaged: not found.
- Any equivalent STOVL or door-cycle limit for a U.S. store (GBU-12, GBU-32, SDB, JSOW): not found.
- The Stack Exchange hover-door observation has no official explanation in the sources retrieved. Cause is not published.
