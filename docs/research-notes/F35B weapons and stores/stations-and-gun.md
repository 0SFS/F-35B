# F-35B weapons stations, internal bays, and gun

Research date: 2026-10-22. Scope is unclassified public material. The simulator models the F-35B only; A and C are included where a public source states a difference.

Status labels used below: **operational** (in service or cleared for fleet use in the source), **flight-tested** (flown in a test event, not claimed as operational), **planned** (roadmap, study, or contract, not flown or fielded in the source), **superseded** (later public statement retires an earlier plan). Forum posts, game manuals, Wikipedia, and unsourced compilations were not used as authorities. Where a secondary outlet is the only available text of a company or government statement, that is marked.

The 2010 and 2012 station-capacity slides are graphic tables. Their PDF text layers scramble left-to-right column order. Capacity figures below are taken from those text layers. Which physical station gets which figure is the consistent reading of those figures plus later prose that names stations 3, 4, 8, and 9. That reading is identified where it is a reading rather than a sentence in the source.

# Internal bays and what they can hold

## How many internal weapons bays does each variant have, and what do public sources say they can hold (station names/numbers, AIM-120 vs bomb vs external-only stores)?

### Takeaway

Each variant has two internal weapons bays and four internal weapon stations. The published air-to-air load in those bays is four AIM-120s, two per bay. A later adapter raises that to six on the A and C only. The B bay is the 1,000 lb-class bay; the A and C bays are the 2,000 lb-class bays. Wingtip stations are air-to-air only. The centerline station is the gun station.

### Cited Findings

- On 17 March 2015, the F-35 deputy program executive (U.S. Navy) briefed the jet as having “four internal and seven external weapon stations.” The same brief: internal bays are “combined A-G and A-A,” “2000 lb on F-35 A and F-35C,” “1000 lb on F-35B,” with “internal adapters.” — [RADM Mahr, Precision Strike Association, 17 March 2015](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2015/PSAR/RADMMahr.pdf)
- Lockheed’s 2010 public-release weapon-system brief uses the same split: “Internal Weapon Bays — Combined A-G and A-A — 2000 lb on CTOL & CV — 1000 lb on STOVL — Internal Adapters,” plus “6 External Hardpoints Fwd of MLG” and a “Centerline Station On All Versions.” Distribution statement A, public release JSF 10-205. — [Doug Hayward, Lockheed Martin, March 2010](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2010/armament/TuesdayLandmarkADougHayward.pdf)
- The 2010 and 2012 station tables both list 11 stations, numbered 1 through 11. Store-type cells are A/A, A/A and A/S, or Gun. Two stations are A/A-only at 350 lb (stations 5 and 7). Station 6 is Gun at 1,000 lb. Station 11 is A/A at 300 lb. The remaining cells are A/A and A/S. — [Hayward, March 2010](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2010/armament/TuesdayLandmarkADougHayward.pdf); [Col Mark Werth, JSF program office, 21 March 2012](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2012/annual_psr/WERTH.pdf)
- Lockheed, quoted by The War Zone, named the internal bay stations that the Sidekick adapter uses: “A new weapons adapter, nicknamed Sidekick, enables the F-35 to carry two AIM-120 sized missiles at internal weapons bay stations 4 and 8.” The adapter “development is on contract and proceeding on schedule.” This is a company statement reported by a secondary outlet; the article date was not in the excerpt retrieved on 2026-10-22. Status in that statement: **planned / on contract**, not described as operational. — [The War Zone, quoting Lockheed Martin](https://www.twz.com/adapter-for-f-35-internal-carriage-of-six-aim-120-missiles-is-progressing)
- At a Lockheed media briefing on 1 May 2019, F-35 test pilot Tony “Brick” Wilson described Sidekick as company-funded then. Each of the two bays on the F-35A and F-35C would carry three AIM-120s instead of two, six internally. The rack “is not compatible with the vertical lift Marine Corps F-35B version, which has smaller weapons bay.” — [Seapower, 1 May 2019 briefing](https://seapowermagazine.org/lockheed-develops-rack-to-make-f-35a-c-a-six-shooter/)
- Greg Ulmer, then Lockheed F-35 general manager, said on 17 June 2019 that Block 4 work included “increasing the internal payload from four weapons to six.” He did not, in that reported remark, say the B was included or excluded. No integration date was set. — [Air & Space Forces Magazine, 17 June 2019](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/lockheed-looking-at-extending-the-f-35s-range-weapons-suite/)
- The 2023 Lockheed product cards for A, B, and C use the same Block 4 sentence: upgrades include “increased missile-carriage capacity.” That boilerplate does not give a missile count or say the B bay changed size. Cards carry PIRA AER2023060207, ©2023. — [F-35A product card](https://www.f35.com/content/dam/lockheed-martin/aero/f35/documents/F-35A%20Product%20Card.pdf); [F-35B product card](https://www.f35.com/content/dam/lockheed-martin/aero/f35/documents/F-35B%20Product%20Card.pdf); [F-35C product card](https://www.f35.com/content/dam/lockheed-martin/aero/f35/documents/F-35C%20Product%20Card.pdf)
- JSF program-office weapons roadmap, 21 March 2012, by variant and block. This is a **2012 plan**, not a 2026 clearance list. The slide states: “Only the F-35B is on contract to have weapons integrated in Block 2.”
  - F-35A: internal gun. External AIM-9X and GBU-12. Block 2 internal: 2× AIM-120C and GBU-12. Block 3 internal: 4× AIM-120C, GBU-31 JDAM 2,000 lb (MK-84 warhead), GBU-31 2,000 lb (BLU-109 warhead), GBU-39 SDB-I.
  - F-35B: missionized gun. External AIM-9X and GBU-12. Block 2 internal: 2× AIM-120C, GBU-12, and GBU-32 JDAM 1,000 lb (MK-83/BLU-110 warhead). Block 3 internal: 4× AIM-120C. The B chart in this roadmap does not list GBU-31 or JSOW.
  - F-35C: missionized gun. External AIM-9X and GBU-12. Internal path shows 2× AIM-120C, then 4× AIM-120C, plus GBU-31 (MK-84 and BLU-109), AGM-154A/C JSOW, and GBU-32.
  - “UK Unique Weapons” on the same roadmap: Paveway IV and AIM-132 ASRAAM. The extracted text does not assign them to a station or to internal versus external. — [Werth, 21 March 2012](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2012/annual_psr/WERTH.pdf)
- The 2015 JPO growth slide lists, as **proposed** Block 4 items rather than then-fielded stores: AIM-9X Block 2, GBU-54/GBU-38, SDB II, JSOW C-1, “expanded internal AA carriage,” JSOW external, UK Meteor, UK SPEAR 3, UK ASRAAM new build, JSM, and others. The brief says integration requests were likely to exceed capacity and that delivery depended on funding and certification. — [Mahr, 17 March 2015](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2015/PSAR/RADMMahr.pdf)
- DOT&E, in the FY2018 F-35 report, describes what Block 3F-or-later software was **intended** to employ, for “the F-35,” without a variant split in that sentence: GBU-12, GBU-31/32 JDAM, GBU-39 SDB, JSOW C-1, AIM-120C, AIM-9X, and the 25 mm GAU-22/A. — [DOT&E FY2018 F-35 report](https://www.dote.osd.mil/Portals/97/pub/reports/FY2018/dod/2018f35jsf.pdf)
- Air & Space Forces Magazine’s F-35A profile, current enough to mention a September 2025 lots 18–19 agreement and full-rate production approval on 12 March 2024, gives the “standard internal loadout” as two AIM-120 AMRAAMs and two GBU-31 JDAMs. That sentence is about the F-35A. The same page says the F-35A can carry up to 22,000 lb “on 10 stations: two internal bays … and/or six wing and fuselage pylons.” “10 stations” disagrees with the JPO’s 11 (4 internal + 7 external). Treat the magazine count as secondary. — [Air & Space Forces Magazine, F-35 Lightning II](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/weapons/f-35/)
- RAF aircraft page for Lightning F-35B lists weapons as Meteor, AMRAAM, ASRAAM, and Paveway IV. The page does not say which are cleared versus intended, and it does not give stations. UK IOC is given as December 2018; IOC (Maritime) December 2020. Page undated; retrieved 2026-10-22. — [Royal Air Force, Lightning F-35B](https://www.raf.mod.uk/aircraft/current-aircraft/lightning-f35b/)
- Meteor on the F-35B was **flight-tested**, not operational, as of the RAF’s 28 February 2025 release: a U.S. Marine Corps F-35B flew an inert Meteor from NAS Patuxent River for environmental data. “The UK is leading the integration campaign for F-35B”; Italy is sponsoring F-35A integration. — [Royal Air Force, 28 February 2025](https://www.raf.mod.uk/news/articles/meteors-first-flight-on-an-f-35b/)
- SPEAR on the F-35B was **flight-tested** in early 2026 and was not a release: MBDA’s 21 May 2026 release says four SPEAR missiles were loaded, “housed in the payload bay,” on a U.S. test F-35B flown from Patuxent River by a Royal Navy test pilot. The release says jettison trials were still ahead. — [MBDA, 21 May 2026](https://www.mbda-systems.com/mbda-spear-first-flight-f-35b)
- The UK Public Accounts Committee reports the department’s biggest F-35 concern as the lack of a standoff weapon, and that SPEAR 3 was “not likely to be in place until the early 2030s,” waiting on U.S.-led TR-3/Block 4 software. That is a **planned** in-service window, not a clearance. — [UK House of Commons Committee of Public Accounts](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm5901/cmselect/cmpubacc/1232/report.html)
- MIL-STD-1760C Class 1 is stated at air-to-ground stations, “tailored Class 1” at air-to-air stations, with 8 independent MIL-STD-1553 channels. Suspension and release equipment is described as non-pyrotechnic. The centerline station is “structural & tailored (STOVL) MIL-STD-1760 Class 1.” These are interface statements from 2010, repeated in substance in 2015. They are not store-clearance lists. — [Hayward, March 2010](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2010/armament/TuesdayLandmarkADougHayward.pdf); [Mahr, 17 March 2015](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2015/PSAR/RADMMahr.pdf)

### Inferences

- Station map that matches every named station in the sources above. Capacities are pounds, as the slides’ payload language is in pounds. CTOL/CV figures are the same in the 2010 and 2012 tables. STOVL figures use the 2010 table; the 2012 conflict is in the next bullet.
  - Station 1, wingtip, A/A only: 300 lb. Pair is station 11.
  - Station 2, outboard wing, A/A and A/S: 2,500 lb CTOL/CV; 1,500 lb STOVL. Pair is station 10.
  - Station 3, inboard wing, A/A and A/S: 5,000 lb all variants. Pair is station 9. These are the stations Ulmer called the inboard pair.
  - Station 4, internal bay, A/A and A/S: 2,500 lb CTOL/CV; 1,500 lb STOVL in the 2010 table. Pair is station 8. These are the stations Lockheed called internal bay stations 4 and 8.
  - Station 5, internal bay, A/A only: 350 lb all variants. Pair is station 7.
  - Station 6, centerline: Gun, 1,000 lb, all variants in the table, including CTOL even though only B and C carry the pod.
- The usual diagram puts station 1 on the port wingtip and station 11 on the starboard wingtip. No sentence in the sources reviewed says “port” or “starboard.” Limits are symmetric, so side does not change a B loadout.
- Baseline internal combinations that follow the 2012 roadmap plus the bay-class sentences, not a single published “loadout card”:
  - All variants, air-to-air, internal: 4× AIM-120 (stations 4, 5, 7, and 8). **Planned for Block 3 in 2012; later treated as the pre-Sidekick internal missile load.**
  - A and C, strike, internal: 2× AIM-120 on the A/A stations plus a 2,000 lb-class bomb (GBU-31) on each A/G station.
  - B, strike, internal: 2× AIM-120 plus a 1,000 lb-class bomb (GBU-32) or 500 lb GBU-12 on the A/G stations. A 2,000 lb GBU-31 is not on the B internal roadmap.
  - A and C only, Sidekick: 6× AIM-120, described as three per bay, by putting two AIM-120-class missiles on stations 4 and 8. **Not for the B. Not shown as operational in the sources reviewed.**
- AIM-9X is drawn on the external side of the 2012 roadmap, which fits the 300 lb wingtip stations. That does not by itself prove a wingtip-only clearance, and it does not prove AIM-9X is forbidden on stations 2/10.
- “External-only” in public documents, as a class rather than a per-store clearance: wingtip A/A stations (too light and typed A/A-only for bombs or tanks); the B’s inability to put a 2,000 lb-class weapon or JSOW inside the bay; Sidekick. Large external stores on the 2010 carriage graphic (JASSM, Storm Shadow, 2,000 lb Paveway III, cluster munitions, a wing tank) were an SDD certification **plan**, and later sources show the tank plan was dropped. Do not treat that 2010 graphic as a cleared B loadout.

### Gaps

- No post-2015 primary sentence was found that repeats “1,000 lb on F-35B.” Nothing found says the B bay was later enlarged. The 2023 product cards do not restate bay class.
- No primary source found for how many GBU-39s fit on one internal station, or for a 14-inch bay-length delta. Those figures appear on unsourced secondary pages and are omitted.
- No primary source found that assigns ASRAAM, Meteor, Paveway IV, or SPEAR to a station number. SPEAR’s 2026 flight carried four missiles in “the payload bay” without saying per station or whether both bays were used.
- No primary source found that JSOW is cleared on the F-35B, internally or externally. It is on the 2012 F-35C internal plan and in the generic Block 3F DOT&E list.
- Sidekick’s operational date, as of 2026-10-22, was not established. The latest primary-adjacent statements in hand are “on contract” (Lockheed via The War Zone) and the 2019 briefing. A 2025–2026 secondary article describes it as still short of fleet operational status; that article was not used as proof.
- Whether stations 4 and 8 on the B can carry an AIM-120 **instead of** a bomb, simultaneously with the AIM-120s on 5 and 7, is the plain reading of “4× AIM-120C” internal on the B Block 3 line. The roadmap does not spell the station pairing out in a sentence.

# F-35B external station map

## What is the public external station map for the F-35B (wing stations, inboard/outboard, centerline if any), including which stations are wet, which can carry the gun pod, and which are used for external tanks?

### Takeaway

The B uses the same 11-station arrangement: three pylons per wing plus a centerline. Stations 3 and 9, the inboard wing pylons, are the ones publicly described as plumbed for fuel. The gun pod is the centerline store, station 6, and the B pod does not fit the C. No public source shows an external tank certified and in service on the B. Early tank sizes disagree with each other, and the requirement was dropped before a 2019 restart that was still only a study.

### Cited Findings

- Six external hardpoints “fwd of MLG,” plus a centerline station “on all versions.” The centerline is the missionized-gun station for STOVL and CV. — [Hayward, March 2010](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2010/armament/TuesdayLandmarkADougHayward.pdf); [Mahr, 17 March 2015](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2015/PSAR/RADMMahr.pdf)
- Ulmer, 17 June 2019: the early plan was 480-gallon drop tanks “on the inboard wing stations,” and “that requirement was dropped” because of internal fuel volume. Wing stations 3 and 9, “the two closest to the fuselage,” are “already piped to accept fuel on the wings.” Lockheed was then studying 600-gallon tanks. “There is no set timeline.” The article’s own narration says “there are 12 stations on the strike fighter for weapons pylons.” That “12” is not inside the quotation marks attributed to Ulmer, and it disagrees with the JPO’s 4+7 count. — [Air & Space Forces Magazine, 17 June 2019](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/lockheed-looking-at-extending-the-f-35s-range-weapons-suite/)
- The 2010 Lockheed carriage graphic’s extracted text includes a “426-Gallon Wing Tank” among stores in the external-weapons overview, in a category headed along the lines of stores to be fully certified during SDD. This is a **plan on a 2009/2010 slide**, and the gallon figure disagrees with Ulmer’s later “480-gallon.” The figure is only as reliable as a text extraction of a graphic. — [Hayward, March 2010](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2010/armament/TuesdayLandmarkADougHayward.pdf)
- The War Zone, 30 June 2025, secondary: external tanks “have never materialized”; Lockheed design studies in 2004–2007 covered 480- and 460-gallon underwing tanks and the 480-gallon design was abandoned after aerodynamic and stores-separation problems; the 460-gallon design was not pursued. The article says the USAF FY2026 budget request funds a feasibility evaluation of external fuel tanks for long-range F-35 missions, and that the outlet was told the work could cover underwing tanks for all three variants. It also reports a JPO statement, given to the outlet in January (read as January 2025 from the article date): “There is no approved program of record requirement for auxiliary internal fuel tanks.” That JPO sentence is about **internal** auxiliary tanks, not a certification of external tanks. — [The War Zone, 30 June 2025](https://www.twz.com/air/plans-to-finally-give-f-35-external-fuel-tanks-emerge-in-new-air-force-bud)
- Gun pod location and non-interchangeability are under the gun question. Short form: B and C, external centerline pod; an F-35B pod cannot be mounted on an F-35C. — [DOT&E FY2018](https://www.dote.osd.mil/Portals/97/pub/reports/FY2018/dod/2018f35jsf.pdf); [DOT&E FY2019](https://www.dote.osd.mil/Portals/97/pub/reports/FY2019/dod/2019f35jsf.pdf?ver=2020-01-30-115432-173)
- STOVL column of the station table rates the outboard wing stations lower than CTOL/CV (1,500 lb versus 2,500 lb) and rates the inboard wing stations at 5,000 lb for STOVL as well as CTOL/CV. Wingtip stations stay 300 lb A/A. — [Hayward, March 2010](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2010/armament/TuesdayLandmarkADougHayward.pdf); [Werth, 21 March 2012](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2012/annual_psr/WERTH.pdf)

### Inferences

- F-35B external map for the simulator, using the station reading in the previous section:
  - 1 and 11: wingtip, dry, A/A only, 300 lb. Not a tank station. Not the gun.
  - 2 and 10: outboard wing, A/A and A/S, 1,500 lb on the B. Not described as plumbed. Not the gun.
  - 3 and 9: inboard wing, A/A and A/S, 5,000 lb on the B, and the only stations a Lockheed program head has publicly called piped for fuel. A tank, if ever cleared, goes here, one per station, in place of a weapon.
  - 6: centerline, gun pod only in the station table. Not described as a tank station.
- “Wet” in the public record means stations 3 and 9 are plumbed. It does not mean a tank is cleared, and it does not mean those stations are unusable for weapons.
- No published F-35B external-tank operating loadout exists in the sources reviewed. Modeling a tank on 3 and/or 9 is a **study configuration**, not an operational one. Gallon count should not be hard-coded as 426, 460, 480, or 600 without a flag: all four numbers appear, none as a fielded B store.

### Gaps

- No official tank capacity, weight, or pylon part number for a certified F-35 store was found. The 426-versus-480-versus-460 disagreement is unresolved; 426 may be an extraction error.
- No source found that any station other than 3 and 9 is plumbed.
- No source found that the centerline can carry a tank, a bomb, or a targeting pod. The table types it as Gun.
- Israeli F-35I range-extension reports were not used. The F-35I is an A-family jet, and the public remarks do not give an F-35B station.

# How A, B, and C differ

## How do F-35A, F-35B, and F-35C internal capacity and external station counts differ?

### Takeaway

Station **count** does not differ: two bays, four internal stations, six wing stations, one centerline, on all three. What differs is internal weapon class, outboard and (in one slide) internal station ratings, gun installation, total weapons-payload figure, and Sidekick. The B is the 1,000 lb internal-bay jet, with 1,500 lb outboard stations, a centerline gun pod, and a 15,000 lb weapons-payload card. A and C are the 2,000 lb internal-bay jets, with 2,500 lb outboard stations and an 18,000 lb card. Only the A has an internal gun.

### Cited Findings

- Same 4 internal + 7 external stations for the family, in a brief that shows A, B, and C together. — [Mahr, 17 March 2015](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2015/PSAR/RADMMahr.pdf)
- Internal weapon class: 2,000 lb on A and C; 1,000 lb on B. — [Hayward, March 2010](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2010/armament/TuesdayLandmarkADougHayward.pdf); [Mahr, 17 March 2015](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2015/PSAR/RADMMahr.pdf); B roadmap shows GBU-32 not GBU-31, while A and C roadmaps show GBU-31 — [Werth, 21 March 2012](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2012/annual_psr/WERTH.pdf)
- Sidekick / six internal AIM-120s: A and C only, because the B bay is smaller. — [Seapower, May 2019](https://seapowermagazine.org/lockheed-develops-rack-to-make-f-35a-c-a-six-shooter/); [The War Zone, quoting Lockheed on stations 4 and 8](https://www.twz.com/adapter-for-f-35-internal-carriage-of-six-aim-120-missiles-is-progressing)
- 2023 product-card weapons payload and the related airframe figures a simulator may otherwise mix up with payload. All three cards are ©2023, PIRA AER2023060207.
  - F-35A: weapons payload 18,000 lb / 8,160 kg; internal fuel 18,250 lb / 8,278 kg; length 51.4 ft; span 35 ft; max g 9.0. — [F-35A product card](https://www.f35.com/content/dam/lockheed-martin/aero/f35/documents/F-35A%20Product%20Card.pdf)
  - F-35B: weapons payload 15,000 lb / 6,800 kg; internal fuel 13,100 lb / 5,942 kg; length 51.2 ft; span 35 ft; max g 7.0. The 15,000 lb figure is the same on the 2015 card and the 2019 card. — [F-35B product card, 2023](https://www.f35.com/content/dam/lockheed-martin/aero/f35/documents/F-35B%20Product%20Card.pdf); [F-35B card, 2015, FG15-1280](https://www.f35.com/content/dam/lockheed-martin/aero/f35/documents/fg15-1280_002_f-35b_pc.pdf); [F-35B card, 2019](https://www.lockheedmartin.com/content/dam/lockheed-martin/aero/documents/F-35/f35B.pdf)
  - F-35C: weapons payload 18,000 lb / 8,160 kg; internal fuel 19,200 lb / 8,708 kg; length 51.5 ft; span 43 ft; wing area 668 ft²; max g 7.5. — [F-35C product card](https://www.f35.com/content/dam/lockheed-martin/aero/f35/documents/F-35C%20Product%20Card.pdf)
- Gun installation differs; see the gun section. A internal, B and C pods that do not interchange. — [DOT&E FY2018](https://www.dote.osd.mil/Portals/97/pub/reports/FY2018/dod/2018f35jsf.pdf)
- Outboard station rating differs: 2,500 lb CTOL/CV versus 1,500 lb STOVL. Inboard 5,000 lb does not differ. Wingtip 300 lb does not differ. Internal A/A stations 350 lb do not differ. — [Hayward, March 2010](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2010/armament/TuesdayLandmarkADougHayward.pdf)
- Lockheed’s 2010 CTOL comparison slide states F-35 CTOL internal fuel as 18,483 lb. That is fuel, on a different slide from the weapons table. The 2023 A card’s 18,250 lb internal fuel does not match 18,483 lb. Neither number is a weapons payload. — [Hayward, March 2010](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2010/armament/TuesdayLandmarkADougHayward.pdf); [F-35A product card, 2023](https://www.f35.com/content/dam/lockheed-martin/aero/f35/documents/F-35A%20Product%20Card.pdf)

### Inferences

- For an F-35B-only model, copy the A/C station **layout**, then apply B limits: no internal GBU-31/JSOW-class store, no Sidekick, outboard stations 1,500 lb, gun only as a removable centerline pod, weapons payload capped by the 15,000 lb card rather than the A/C 18,000 lb card or the 22,000 lb marketing figure.
- External station **count** should be 6 wing + 1 centerline for the B, not “fewer pylons than the A.” Public sources do not give the B fewer hardpoints. They give the B lower ratings and a shorter bay.
- The 2010 stealth-features slide lists CTOL 18,500 lb, STOVL 14,000 lb, and CV 20,000 lb beside “Large Capacity Internal Fuel Tanks,” and the next slide’s CTOL internal fuel is 18,483 lb. Those three round numbers are internal-fuel callouts, not the weapons payload. Using 14,000 lb as the B weapons payload would contradict the product card’s 15,000 lb and the fuel card’s 13,100 lb.

### Gaps

- No public per-variant count of “usable” stations after the gun and bays are accounted for, beyond 4 internal + 7 external.
- No source found for a different external-pylon count on the B caused by the lift fan or roll posts. Roll posts are called out on the STOVL airframe slide; they are not described there as deleting a pylon. — [Hayward, March 2010](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2010/armament/TuesdayLandmarkADougHayward.pdf)
- The magazine’s “10 stations” and the 2019 article’s “12 stations” are unresolved. The JPO sentence “four internal and seven external” is the one to model.

# Gun

## What gun does each variant have, where is it mounted on the B, and what rate of fire, ammunition capacity, caliber, muzzle velocity, or dispersion is published?

### Takeaway

All three variants use the 25 mm four-barrel GAU-22/A. On the B it is not internal: it is in a centerline missionized pod that does not fit the C. Published installation figures are 220 rounds in the pod, 181 rounds in the A’s internal gun, and a fleet statement of up to 50 rounds per second. The gun maker’s 2024 sheet says up to 3,300 shots per minute, which is not the same number. Muzzle velocity and a gun-level dispersion are published by the manufacturer. Installed F-35B dispersion, in milliradians at the aircraft, is not published; DOT&E says the B and C pods met the contract specification and the A’s internal gun did not. The numeric contract specification is not in the public DOT&E text.

### Cited Findings

- Gun type and where it is mounted:
  - “GAU-22/A 25mm Gun — Internal (CTOL) — Missionized (STOVL/CV).” “Internal 25mm 4-Barrel Gattling Gun” is the CTOL callout. “Centerline Gun Pod with 25mm Gun” is the STOVL and CV callout. — [Hayward, March 2010](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2010/armament/TuesdayLandmarkADougHayward.pdf)
  - “GAU-22 25mm Gun — Internal F-35A — Missionized F-35B & F-35C.” — [Mahr, 17 March 2015](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2015/PSAR/RADMMahr.pdf)
  - “All three F-35 variants have the GAU-22/A cannon. The F-35A gun is internal; the F-35B and F-35C each use an external gun pod. Differences in the outer mold-line fairing mounting make the gun pods unique to a specific variant (i.e., an F-35B gun pod cannot be mounted on an F-35C aircraft).” Repeated in the FY2019 report. — [DOT&E FY2018](https://www.dote.osd.mil/Portals/97/pub/reports/FY2018/dod/2018f35jsf.pdf); [DOT&E FY2019](https://www.dote.osd.mil/Portals/97/pub/reports/FY2019/dod/2019f35jsf.pdf?ver=2020-01-30-115432-173)
- General Dynamics Ordnance and Tactical Systems product sheet, document 400001948, marked 2024.08, file path dated September 2024. This is the **gun**, and the sheet says it was developed for both the internal and external F-35 installations:
  - Four-barrel, 25 mm, externally powered Gatling. Hydraulic drive. Linkless feed.
  - Weight 230 lb (104.3 kg).
  - Rate of fire “up to 3,300 shots per minute.”
  - Dispersion “5 milliradians diameter, 80% circle (1.4 milliradians, 1-sigma radius).”
  - Muzzle velocity, TP and HEI ammunition: 3,560 ft/s (1,085 m/s). API ammunition: 3,400 ft/s (1,036 m/s).
  - Average recoil force 3,700 lb (16.5 kN).
  - Gun envelope on the sheet: length 83.2 in (2,113 mm); other stated dimensions 10.2 in (259.6 mm), 15.6 in (396.2 mm), 11.1 in (281.6 mm), and 13.3 in (337.5 mm). The sheet does not label which of those is height versus width in a sentence; the drawing labels them separately.
  - “Over 40 pounds lighter and occupies 20 percent less volume than the comparably equipped 5-barrel counterpart” (the GAU-12/U). — [GD-OTS GAU-22/A sheet, 2024.08](https://www.gdots.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/400001948-GAU-22-A-2024-09-V04.pdf)
- Installation-specific rate, which disagrees with “up to 3,300”:
  - Lockheed, March 2010: “3,000 SPM Four Barrel Derivative of GAU-12.” Internal CTOL feed: “181 Rounds Linear Linkless Feed System.” Pod: “220 Rounds Helical Feed System.” Qualified at that time with PGU-20/U API and PGU-23 TP. “Primary Mission A/G with A/A Fallout Capability.” Qualification of the gun “completed in 2008”; the pod was then “in 36,000 Round Qualification Test Program.” — [Hayward, March 2010](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2010/armament/TuesdayLandmarkADougHayward.pdf)
  - General Dynamics missionized-gun brief, 2010: GAU-22/A, “3000 spm, 25-mm, 4-barrel, reverse clearing, GAU-12U derivative.” Dispersion “5 milliradians diameter, 80 percent circle.” Ammunition handling system “220-rnd helical.” Pod “1017 lb fully loaded.” Pod size “27 in wide, 32 in high, 146 in long.” Hydraulic system “4000-psi.” Pod structure by Terma. Gun System Control Unit by Hamilton Sundstrand. A “220-rnd fire out” was a listed specialty test. Engineering testing of the missionized system was “completed May 2009” (13,503 rounds fired, 1,575 rounds cycled). Qualification-complete was an upcoming milestone dated June 2010 in that brief, so the June 2010 date is a **plan as of the briefing**, not a completion report. Ground-vibration fit on aircraft 2BF:003 at Lockheed, July 2009, “fit perfectly.” — [Douglas Parker, General Dynamics, 2010 NDIA](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2010/armament/TuesdayLandmarkADouglasParker.pdf)
  - U.S. Air Force Air Combat Command, 24 August 2018, on the **F-35A** after the first operational load-and-fire training at Hill AFB on 13 August 2018: “The F-35A’s internal cannon carries 181, 25-millimeter rounds and can fire at a rate up to 50 rounds per second.” Fifty rounds per second is 3,000 rounds per minute. This is an operational-unit statement about the A, not the B pod. — [Air Combat Command, 24 August 2018](https://www.acc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/1616527/388th-fw-dialing-in-cannon-loading-firing-in-training/)
- Accuracy, installed, from DOT&E. The contract’s numeric accuracy limit is **not** printed in these reports.
  - FY2017 report: F-35A air-to-ground accuracy testing showed a consistent “long and to-the-right aiming bias.” — [DOT&E FY2017 F-35 report](https://www.dote.osd.mil/Portals/97/pub/reports/FY2017/dod/2017f35jsf.pdf)
  - FY2018 report, testing through September 2018: DOT&E considered F-35A installed accuracy “unacceptable.” SDD accuracy “failed to meet the contract specification.” Software changes improved “stability of gun aiming cues”; “no software or hardware corrections have yet been implemented to correct the gun accuracy errors.” Gun-mount investigations “revealed misalignments that result in muzzle alignment errors,” so “the true alignment of each F-35A gun is not known.” Intermittent “unsafe gun” cockpit alerts on two F-35A aircraft during air-to-air gun testing; cause then under investigation. “F-35B and F-35C air-to-ground accuracy results to date with the gun pod have been consistent and meet the contract specifications. They do not show the accuracy errors of the internal gun on the F-35A.” Activity through July 2018: 13 pod strafe missions (BF-15, BF-16, BF-17, CF-08), 2,695 rounds of PGU-23 and PGU-32, some for accuracy compliance. F-35A: 19 missions, more than 3,400 rounds, including PGU-23, PGU-47, and PGU-48. — [DOT&E FY2018](https://www.dote.osd.mil/Portals/97/pub/reports/FY2018/dod/2018f35jsf.pdf)
  - FY2019 report: F-35A installed accuracy still “unacceptable” until new hardware and software were verified; verification was not complete. Newer F-35As had cracks in outer-mold-line coatings and the underlying chine longeron skin “near the gun muzzle” after gun employment. “The U.S. Air Force has restricted the gun to combat use only for production Lot 9 and newer aircraft.” B and C pod air-to-ground results “have been consistent and meet the contract specifications.” — [DOT&E FY2019](https://www.dote.osd.mil/Portals/97/pub/reports/FY2019/dod/2019f35jsf.pdf?ver=2020-01-30-115432-173)
- Rounds named in those DOT&E gun sections: PGU-23 (training practice), PGU-32/U semi-armor-piercing high-explosive incendiary, PGU-47/U armor-piercing high-explosive incendiary with tracer, PGU-48/B frangible armor-piercing. Air-to-ground flight lethality tests of PGU-32/U, PGU-47/U, and PGU-48/B were flown from all three variants at China Lake, August–December 2017. “The target damage results are classified.” — [DOT&E FY2018](https://www.dote.osd.mil/Portals/97/pub/reports/FY2018/dod/2018f35jsf.pdf)
- The missionized pod’s component list includes a “Weapon Bay Door bumper,” blast deflector, purge-air inlet and exhaust, and loading door. The bumper is a published part of the pod, which places the pod next to the bay doors by design. It is not a door-timing or clearance-dimension disclosure. — [Parker, 2010](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2010/armament/TuesdayLandmarkADouglasParker.pdf)
- Station 6 capacity in the 2010 and 2012 tables is 1,000 lb. The 2010 pod brief says the loaded missionized gun system is 1,017 lb. Both are published. They differ by 17 lb. — [Hayward, March 2010](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2010/armament/TuesdayLandmarkADougHayward.pdf); [Parker, 2010](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2010/armament/TuesdayLandmarkADouglasParker.pdf)

### Inferences

- Model the B gun as a removable centerline store on station 6, GAU-22/A, 25 mm, 220 rounds, linkless helical feed, hydraulically driven. Do not model an internal B gun or an A-style muzzle door on the B.
- Prefer the installation rate of 3,000 rounds per minute (Lockheed 2010, GD installation brief 2010, and the USAF’s “50 rounds per second” for the A) over the 2024 gun-sheet “up to 3,300,” unless a later installation document is found. The sheet’s “up to” is a gun capability, not a statement of the F-35 fire-control setting. **Not published: which of the two rates the B pod uses in service.**
- 220 rounds at 3,000 rounds per minute is 4.4 seconds if the entire magazine is fired in one continuous burst. That arithmetic is not a published burst limit. Parker only records that a 220-round fire-out was a test point.
- 181 rounds is the published A magazine (Lockheed 2010 and USAF 2018). Some secondary writeups say 180. No primary 180-round figure was found. Do not use 181 for the B pod.
- Manufacturer dispersion (5 mrad diameter, 80% circle) is the gun specification, repeated in 2010 and 2024. DOT&E’s finding that the B/C **pods met the contract spec** and the A **installation did not** means aircraft alignment can dominate the gun spec. For the B, a model can use the gun spec as the published dispersion and should not add the A’s long-and-right bias to the pod.
- Muzzle velocities on the 2024 sheet are by ammunition type (TP/HEI versus API), not a flight-test measurement from an F-35. No separate installed muzzle velocity was found.
- Loaded pod mass 1,017 lb exceeds the slide’s 1,000 lb station-6 cell. Treat 1,000 lb as a rounded slide rating and 1,017 lb as the only published loaded-system weight. Empty-pod weight was not found (the 230 lb figure is the gun alone).
- The pod is missionized: support equipment in the 2010 brief (hoisting beam, mount rail, ground handling adapter, loader) is installation tooling. Modeling it as always welded on would contradict “missionized” and that tooling. Modeling it as interchangeable with the C pod would contradict DOT&E.

### Gaps

- No published F-35B or F-35C operational round count other than the 220-round helical system. No USAF-style “50 rounds per second” sentence was found for the pod.
- No published installed dispersion number, in milliradians, for the B pod. “Meets the contract specification” is the public result. The specification value was not found.
- No published statement of which side of the F-35A fuselage the internal gun is on, in the official documents opened here. FY2019 places A-model cracking “near the gun muzzle” in the chine longeron skin, which puts the muzzle in the chine, not in a wing. Port-versus-starboard was not in that sentence. Secondary outlets say “above the left engine intake”; that was not confirmed from a primary page in this pass.
- Pod designations GPU-9/A (B) and GPU-8/A (C) appear in a secondary article citing a contracting notice and a February 2024 DOT&E text. The FY2018 and FY2019 DOT&E reports state the pods are unique and not interchangeable, and they do **not** use those designations. The designations are unverified here. — [The War Zone, secondary](https://www.twz.com/air/f-35b-and-f-35c-use-different-25mm-gun-pods-unique-to-each-variant)
- No DOT&E annual-report passage reviewed for FY2021 restates the gun. Whether the F-35A accuracy and Lot 9 “combat use only” restriction were later lifted was not established from a primary source in this pass. It does not change the B pod.
- Fuze design and classified lethality results were not collected. Do not invent effect, time of flight, or dispersion growth with range.

# Weights, clearances, and beast mode

## What published station weight limits, store clearance limits, or beast-mode external loadouts exist?

### Takeaway

Station ratings are published as a family table, with a STOVL column. The only clearance statement found is qualitative: internal bays have defined stay-out volumes for the weapon, fall path, maintainer access, and flow field. No inch values were found. “Beast mode” totals of 5,700 lb internal and 22,000 lb combined are F-35A marketing and training descriptions. The B’s published weapons payload is 15,000 lb. No B beast-mode loadout with a station list was found.

### Cited Findings

- Station capacity table, pounds, public release. CTOL/CV and STOVL, from the March 2010 Lockheed slide, in the station reading given above:
  - 1 and 11: 300, all variants, A/A.
  - 2 and 10: 2,500 CTOL/CV; 1,500 STOVL; A/A and A/S.
  - 3 and 9: 5,000, all variants; A/A and A/S.
  - 4 and 8: 2,500 CTOL/CV; 1,500 STOVL in this 2010 slide; A/A and A/S.
  - 5 and 7: 350, all variants; A/A.
  - 6: 1,000, all variants; Gun.
  - Slide headline on the same page: “Over 18K lbs Of Payload Capability.” That headline is not broken out by variant. — [Hayward, March 2010](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2010/armament/TuesdayLandmarkADougHayward.pdf)
- The 21 March 2012 JPO slide repeats “Over 18K lbs Of Payload Capability” and the same CTOL/CV numbers. Its STOVL number groups are not identical to 2010: the extracted STOVL groups include two 2,500 lb cells where a symmetric reading puts stations 4 and 8 at 2,500 lb and leaves stations 2 and 10 at 1,500 lb. The 2010 STOVL groups put four stations at 1,500 lb (2, 4, 8, and 10). Both slides still say, in prose, that the STOVL bay weapon class is 1,000 lb and the CTOL/CV class is 2,000 lb. — [Werth, 21 March 2012](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2012/annual_psr/WERTH.pdf)
- Stay-out volumes, March 2010, no dimensions: “Weapon Stay-Out Volumes Defined to Protect Internal Bay Volume. Includes All Required Weapons Plus Static, Fall and Maintainer Access Clearances. … Additional Clearance for Access and Flow Field Effects.” External: “External Station Spacing and Capacity Sized for Larger External Stores.” Mechanical interface: MIL-STD-8591. No clearance in inches, and no maximum store diameter or length, is printed in the extracted text. — [Hayward, March 2010](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2010/armament/TuesdayLandmarkADougHayward.pdf)
- Variant weapons-payload cards: B 15,000 lb; A and C 18,000 lb. See the comparison section for the card citations. These are not station-by-station sums.
- Beast mode, F-35A, not B:
  - f35.com, reporting RAAF F-35As at Arnhem Thunder 21 with four inert GBU-12s under the wings plus an unspecified internal load: a 2017 Lockheed graphic contrasted “stealth mode” at 5,700 lb carried internally with “beast mode” at 22,000 lb of combined internal and external weapons. — [f35.com, RAAF beast-mode article](https://www.f35.com/f35/news-and-features/raaf-flies-f35as-in-beastmode-for-the-first-time.html)
  - U.S. Air Force, 63rd Fighter Squadron, Luke AFB, configuration flown 22 July 2020 and called beast mode by the unit: six inert 500 lb GBU-12s (four on the wings, two in the weapons bay) and one AIM-9X training missile. The article says this is a training configuration so students see a heavy bring-back, not that it is the structural maximum. Aircraft is the F-35A. — [Air Education and Training Command, 63rd FS](https://www.aetc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/2303929/63rd-fs-implements-beast-mode-weapons-configuration-on-f-35a/)
- Loaded gun pod 1,017 lb versus station 6’s 1,000 lb cell, already cited under the gun.

### Inferences

- For the B, enforce station ratings from the STOVL column, not the CTOL column. A 2,000 lb-class store can sit on an inboard wing station (5,000 lb rating) and still be illegal in the bay (1,000 lb class).
- The 2010 versus 2012 STOVL internal-station rating (1,500 lb versus 2,500 lb on stations 4 and 8) should be carried as an open conflict. It does not lift the 1,000 lb weapon-class limit. A 1,000 lb GBU-32 fits under either rating. A 2,000 lb GBU-31 fits under a 2,500 lb rating on paper and still contradicts the bay-class sentences and the B weapons roadmap.
- Do not use 22,000 lb or 5,700 lb as F-35B limits. Those figures are tied to F-35A graphics and an A training load. The B figure to use as the published total is 15,000 lb.
- “Over 18,000 lb” on the 2010/2012 station slides is a family headline on a page that also shows the B’s lower station ratings. It is a poor match to the later B card and should not override 15,000 lb.
- The Luke loadout is evidence that a bay door-closed carriage of two internal GBU-12s plus four external GBU-12s and a wingtip AIM-9X has been flown on an A. It is not a B clearance, and it leaves stations empty (no second wingtip missile, no inboard 2,000 lb stores, no gun pod).

### Gaps

- No published inch, millimeter, or degree clearance between stores, between a store and the door, or between the gun pod and an open bay door.
- No published F-35B beast-mode photograph caption or load list with station numbers was found.
- No published maximum store weight that sums the STOVL column to the 15,000 lb card. The card and the station table were not tied together by Lockheed in the documents reviewed.
- External-tank clearance and separation limits were not published. Ulmer said separation and signature were still being analyzed in 2019; the 2025 budget item, as reported second-hand, is still a feasibility study.

# Bay doors

## What is publicly known about weapons-bay door operation (number of doors, that they must open to launch, any published timing)?

### Takeaway

Public sources say the doors open, the weapon leaves, and the doors close. They do not publish door count per bay, hinge geometry, angle, or time. The B was flown with bay doors open during early testing, and a 2026 SPEAR flight carried four missiles in the bay, which requires the doors to have been closed for takeoff and the weapons to have been inside. No open/close time was found. Do not invent kinematics.

### Cited Findings

- Two internal bays are the standard description (four internal stations across those bays). See Mahr and Hayward, cited above.
- Lockheed Martin channel video of Maj. Melanie Kusner, F-35 demo team, describing the F-35A and the family’s internal bays: weapons are housed in the weapons bay; when the pilot commands release, “those doors will open, weapons will come out, and the doors will close back up again.” No duration, angle, or door count. — [Lockheed Martin video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5XXgu5fX864)
- The U.S. Air Force demo team has a named maneuver, “Weapons Bay Door Pass,” explained by the F-35A demo pilot. The available description does not include timing or door count. — [U.S. Air Force demo note](https://www.usfk.mil/What-We-Do/Exercises/Freedom-Shield/dvpTag/BEO/dvpmoduleid/139920/videoid/756243/)
- MBDA, 21 May 2026: four SPEAR missiles were loaded in the F-35B payload bay before a flight from Patuxent River. That is captive carriage inside the bay, not a published door cycle. Jettison trials were still to come. — [MBDA, 21 May 2026](https://www.mbda-systems.com/mbda-spear-first-flight-f-35b)
- 2012 JSF flight-sciences objectives included, as **tests still to complete**, “Complete 1 GBU-31 and 1 AIM-120 Separation” on CTOL and “Complete Initial Weapon Release — 1 GBU-12 & 1 GBU-32 Internal” in the STOVL block of that slide. These are separation events, not door timers. — [Werth, 21 March 2012](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2012/annual_psr/WERTH.pdf)
- Release hardware is “non-pyrotechnic” suspension and release equipment. No actuator type, pressure, or stroke is given. — [Hayward, March 2010](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2010/armament/TuesdayLandmarkADougHayward.pdf); [Mahr, 17 March 2015](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2015/PSAR/RADMMahr.pdf)
- The gun pod includes a weapon-bay-door bumper. That is evidence the pod and the doors were designed to meet. It is not a statement that the doors open to a measured angle with the pod installed. — [Parker, 2010](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2010/armament/TuesdayLandmarkADouglasParker.pdf)
- Internal AIM-120s and bombs are released from inside the bay, so the door has to be out of the way for the store to leave. The demo-pilot sentence is the public operational description of that sequence. No source reviewed gives seconds.

### Inferences

- Model one open/close cycle around an internal release: doors shut in the captive, stealth configuration; doors open before the store is allowed to leave; doors close after. That sequence is published in ordinary language. Timing, angular rate, and whether both bays must open together are not published and should be parameters, not facts.
- “Doors” plural means more than one door on the airplane. It does not establish one versus two doors per bay. Photographs exist; this pass did not use a photo as a measured door count.
- A claim on a secondary photo blog that an F-35B was shown with dummy 2,000 lb JDAMs in the open bays conflicts with the 1,000 lb B bay rating and was not used.

### Gaps

- Door count per bay, hinge line, opening angle, opening time, closing time, and whether the A/A station and the A/G station in one bay share one door: not published in the sources reviewed.
- No published limit on speed, angle of attack, or load factor for door operation.
- No published statement that the doors can or cannot reach their open position with the gun pod installed. The bumper says the interference was designed for. It does not give the resulting door travel.

# Station conflicts on the F-35B

## Which stations conflict (tank vs missile vs gun pod) on the F-35B?

### Takeaway

Conflicts that are actually published are station-type and station-rating conflicts: one store per station, tanks only where the fuel plumbing is (3 and 9), gun pod only on the centerline and only the B pod, wingtips and the inner bay stations too light and too specialized for bombs or tanks, and the B bay too small for a 2,000 lb-class weapon or for Sidekick. No source says the gun pod forbids a tank, or that a tank forbids an internal missile. Those are different stations.

### Cited Findings

- Stations 3 and 9 are the inboard pair and the pair described as piped for fuel. A tank on one of them occupies that station. — [Air & Space Forces Magazine, Ulmer, 17 June 2019](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/lockheed-looking-at-extending-the-f-35s-range-weapons-suite/)
- No F-35 variant had a fielded external tank as of that 2019 statement (“requirement was dropped”; 600-gallon tanks still under study, no timeline). As of secondary reporting on 30 June 2025, U.S. external tanks were still a feasibility item, not a cleared store. — [Air & Space Forces Magazine, 17 June 2019](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/lockheed-looking-at-extending-the-f-35s-range-weapons-suite/); [The War Zone, 30 June 2025](https://www.twz.com/air/plans-to-finally-give-f-35-external-fuel-tanks-emerge-in-new-air-force-bud)
- Station table store types: 1 and 11 A/A only at 300 lb; 5 and 7 A/A only at 350 lb; 6 Gun at 1,000 lb; 2, 3, 4, 8, 9, and 10 A/A and A/S. STOVL outboard (2 and 10) 1,500 lb. — [Hayward, March 2010](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2010/armament/TuesdayLandmarkADougHayward.pdf)
- Gun pod is centerline, B-specific fairing, 220 rounds, about 1,017 lb loaded, and physically next to the bay doors (door bumper). B pod “cannot be mounted on an F-35C.” — [Parker, 2010](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2010/armament/TuesdayLandmarkADouglasParker.pdf); [DOT&E FY2018](https://www.dote.osd.mil/Portals/97/pub/reports/FY2018/dod/2018f35jsf.pdf)
- Sidekick, which would put two AIM-120-class missiles on internal stations 4 and 8, “is not compatible” with the B. — [Seapower, May 2019](https://seapowermagazine.org/lockheed-develops-rack-to-make-f-35a-c-a-six-shooter/); [The War Zone, quoting Lockheed](https://www.twz.com/adapter-for-f-35-internal-carriage-of-six-aim-120-missiles-is-progressing)
- B internal weapon class 1,000 lb, so a GBU-31-class store conflicts with the bay even when the inboard **wing** station is rated 5,000 lb. — [Mahr, 17 March 2015](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2015/PSAR/RADMMahr.pdf); [Werth, 21 March 2012](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2012/annual_psr/WERTH.pdf)

### Inferences

- Hard conflicts to enforce on the B, each one a single station or a published type limit:
  - Station 3: weapon **or** fuel tank, not both. Same for station 9. Tank is not a cleared operational store.
  - Stations 1 and 11: A/A store within 300 lb. Not a tank, not a bomb, not the gun.
  - Stations 2 and 10: one A/A or A/S store within 1,500 lb. Not described as wet. A store cleared only against the A’s 2,500 lb outboard rating may not fit the B rating.
  - Stations 5 and 7: one A/A store within 350 lb. Not a bomb station.
  - Stations 4 and 8: one A/S store within the STOVL internal rating, or an AIM-120 as part of the four-missile internal load. Not two AIM-120s on the same station (no Sidekick). Not a 2,000 lb-class bomb.
  - Station 6: B gun pod or empty. Not a tank. Not a C gun pod. Not a wing store moved to the centerline.
- Not conflicts, on the evidence in hand:
  - Gun pod plus internal AIM-120s or 1,000 lb-class bombs. Different stations. The pod was designed with a bay-door bumper. No published “pod deletes the bay” rule was found.
  - Gun pod plus tanks on 3 and 9. Different stations. No published mutual exclusion was found. Both are external and both would be non-stealth or less-stealth configurations; that is a signature statement, not a fit statement. Ulmer discussed tank signature in 2019 without mentioning the pod.
  - Wingtip missiles plus inboard tanks. Different stations. No published exclusion was found.
- A bomb on station 4 conflicts with using station 4 as an AIM-120 station. That is the ordinary one-store-per-station rule, and it is how the roadmap’s “2 AIM-120 + bombs” versus “4 AIM-120” internal loads fit in one bay.

### Gaps

- No published store-to-store clearance conflict (tank on station 3 versus missile on station 2, or pod versus open door) with a go/no-go rule.
- No published rule that the gun pod and a full internal load have been cleared together, either. Absence of a prohibition is not a clearance.
- No published ASRAAM-versus-AIM-9X station conflict. Both are wing A/A missiles in the public record; whether they share station 1/11 or 2/10 on the UK B was not found.
- No published weight-sum conflict that says, for example, two tanks plus the gun pod plus internal bombs exceeds the 15,000 lb card. The card was not accompanied by a worked loadout.
