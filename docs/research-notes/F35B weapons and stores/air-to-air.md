# F-35 air-to-air weapons, with F-35B priority

Scope is public, unclassified material as of 22 October 2026, for a civilian flight simulator: identity, guidance class, size and mass, carriage, variant and block status, and published range. Seeker internals, frequencies, counter-countermeasures, warhead fill, fuze design, and engagement doctrine are omitted on purpose. Where a current official sheet withholds a number, the entry is “not published.” Marketing claims, flight-test events, and operational clearances are labeled separately. Pages with no printed date are marked by the date they were fetched.

## Which AIM-120 variants are carried internally and externally, and what is the F-35B internal count versus a full external load?

### Takeaway
Public sheets treat AMRAAM as cleared on all three F-35 variants and as the only radar-guided air-to-air missile cleared to fly on the jet, but they do not publish a variant-by-station matrix for AIM-120B versus C versus D. A Breaking Defense infographic lists four AIM-120s as an F-35B internal option, the same pure air-to-air internal count it lists for the F-35A and F-35C, and lists two AIM-9Xs as the external air-to-air option. That graphic does not publish a larger external AIM-120 count. AIM-260 is a Lockheed follow-on that the company says will be used on the F-35; it is not a cleared F-35 store in the sources below.

### Cited Findings
- U.S. Navy fact file, last updated 23 September 2021: AMRAAM variants AIM-120A/B/C/D “are operational on U.S. Navy, Marine Corps, and Air Force aircraft.” Platforms include Navy/USMC F-35B/C and Air Force F-35A. The AIM-120C series began deliveries in 1996. Joint procurement of the AIM-120D series began in fiscal 2006. AIM-120C-7 reached IOC in FY2008. The Navy achieved IOC of AIM-120D in January 2015. The file does not say which dash number is cleared in an F-35 bay, and it does not separate internal from external stations. — [U.S. Navy AIM-120 fact file](https://www.navy.mil/Resources/Fact-Files/Display-FactFiles/Article/2168352/aim-120-advanced-medium-range-air-to-air-missile-amraam/)
- NAVAIR product page, fetched 22 October 2026: AMRAAM “is used on” Air Force F-35A and Navy/Marine Corps F-35B/C, among other types. The page gives one guidance label, “Active radar,” and marks speed and range “Classified.” It does not break out B, C, or D by aircraft or station. — [NAVAIR AMRAAM](https://www.navair.navy.mil/product/AMRAAM)
- RTX/Raytheon product page, fetched 22 October 2026: “The newest version of AMRAAM is operational on all F-35 Joint Strike Fighter variants. It is the only radar-guided, air-to-air missile cleared to fly on the F-35.” A separate photo caption identifies an “AMRAAM F3R missile (AIM-120D-3)” launched from an F/A-18F, not from an F-35. The page does not explicitly tie the phrase “newest version” on the F-35 to the AIM-120D-3 suffix. This is a manufacturer claim. — [RTX AMRAAM](https://www.rtx.com/raytheon/what-we-do/air/amraam-missile)
- DOT&E FY2023 AMRAAM report: F-35A/B/C are among the aircraft that “can all employ AMRAAM.” AIM-120D3 SIP-3F finished integrated testing in May 2023, and the fielding recommendation was still pending Service-level approval as of the fourth quarter of FY2023. The report describes D3 as a form-fit-function hardware refresh of the AIM-120D family. It does not state that D3 was fleet-cleared on the F-35B. — [DOT&E FY2023 AMRAAM](https://www.dote.osd.mil/Portals/97/pub/reports/FY2023/af/2023amraam.pdf)
- Air Force Operational Test and Evaluation Center, 14 November 2023: an F-35 live-fire event on developmental software 30R08.051 employed AIM-120D, AIM-120D3, AIM-9X, and AIM-9X-3. “This event constituted the first time an F-35 fired the new AIM-120D3,” and that shot was from an F-35C. The write-up is an operational test of developmental software, not a fleet-release notice for every variant. — [AFOTEC, 14 November 2023](https://www.afotec.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/3587984/uott-conducts-8-live-fire-missile-tests/)
- DOT&E F-35 report covering 2017 test activity (text read from a public copy of that report): with Block 3F software the F-35 “is intended to employ” air-to-air missiles including “AIM-120C” and “AIM-9X.” Block 3F weapons-delivery-accuracy events included AMRAAM shots in 2016 and 2017, scored Successful, Partially Successful, or Unsuccessful by event. The report uses “AIM-120C” and does not print a C-5, C-7, or D suffix in that sentence. — [FY2017 DOT&E F-35 report copy](http://www.aereo.jor.br/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/F-35-report-2017.pdf)
- UK Ministry of Defence written answer, Jeremy Quin, answered 12 July 2022 (UIN 31755): “Currently the UK F-35B Lightning is cleared to employ the Paveway IV Precision Guided Munition, the Advanced Medium Range Air to Air Missile (AMRAAM) and the Advanced Short Range Air to Air Missile (ASRAAM).” The answer says “AMRAAM” with no dash number and no station count. — [UK Parliament UIN 31755](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2022-07-06/31755)
- Breaking Defense infographic `MCM057_infographic_R8.pdf` (undated; “Information sourced by Breaking Defense Editorial and Research”): internal weapons capacity is 5,400 lb for F-35A, 3,500 lb for F-35B, and 5,400 lb for F-35C. Internal options for the F-35B column are 4 AIM-120; 2 AIM-120 and 2 GBU-12; 2 AIM-120 and 2 GBU-32; 2 AIM-120 and 2 GBU-49; and 2 AIM-120 and 8 StormBreakers, the last marked planned integration. F-35A and F-35C columns also list 4 AIM-120 internally. F-35A adds an internal GAU-22 and 2,000 lb-class GBU-31 pairings. F-35B’s column does not list GBU-31, JSOW, JSM, or eight SDB-I internally. External options printed for the F-35B are 2 AIM-9X, 4 GBU-12, 4 GBU-49, 2 JSOW, and 1 GAU-22 25 mm cannon. Internal-plus-external capacity is 15,000 lb for the B and 18,000 lb for the A and C. The external list does not include a count of external AIM-120s. This is an editorial graphic, not a military flight-release document. — [Breaking Defense F-35 infographic PDF](https://cdn2.hubspot.net/hubfs/2097098/F-35_infographic_R8.pdf)
- Lockheed Martin F-35B product card, copyright 2023, public-release marking AER2023060207: weapons payload 15,000 lb / 6,800 kg, matching the infographic’s combined F-35B figure. The card says Block 4 includes “increased missile-carriage capacity” and does not give a missile count or say the increase applies to the B. — [Lockheed F-35B product card, 2023](https://www.f35.com/content/dam/lockheed-martin/aero/f35/documents/F-35B%20Product%20Card.pdf)
- UPI, 3 May 2019, quoting Lockheed Martin: a rack called Sidekick lets each internal bay on the F-35A and F-35C carry three AIM-120s instead of two, for six instead of four. “They are not compatible with the Marine Corps F-35B version, because of its smaller weapons bay.” The same account says A and C racks at that time carried two AIM-120s, or one AIM-120 and a large bomb. This is a company announcement of a rack in development, not a 2026 fleet clearance. — [UPI, 3 May 2019](https://www.upi.com/Defense-News/2019/05/03/Lockheeds-Sidekick-adds-increased-firepower-to-F-35-fighters/6201556888721/)
- The War Zone, quoting a later Lockheed statement: “A new weapons adapter, nicknamed Sidekick, enables the F-35 to carry two AIM-120 sized missiles at internal weapons bay stations 4 and 8.” Lockheed said adapter development was on contract. The article states Sidekick applies to A and C because the F-35B bay is smaller. The article does not report operational clearance. — [The War Zone on Sidekick](https://www.twz.com/adapter-for-f-35-internal-carriage-of-six-aim-120-missiles-is-progressing)
- NAVAIR photo release, 21 June 2012: F-35B test aircraft BF-2 flew with an inert AIM-9X on the starboard pylon, a centerline 25 mm gun pod, and a GBU-32 and an AIM-120 in the starboard weapon bay. That shows early flight-test geometry (missile in the bay, AIM-9X on a wing pylon). It is not a clearance of a maximum load. — [NAVAIR, 21 June 2012](https://www.navair.navy.mil/node/17256)
- Air & Space Forces Magazine AIM-260 page (page image path dated 2026/06; fetched 22 October 2026): contractor Lockheed Martin; integration listed as F-22, with F-35 and collaborative combat aircraft planned; IOC listed as not achieved (2022 had been the initial aim); dimensions “Unknown; sized to fifth-generation fighter’s internal weapons bays.” Several other technical fields on that page are explicitly hedged with “likely.” — [Air & Space Forces Magazine, AIM-260](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/weapons/aim-260-jatm/)
- Reuters, 17 September 2026: Lockheed Martin said the AIM-260 “will be used on stealth fighters like the F-22 Raptor and F-35,” and described greater range than existing air-to-air missiles without a number. That is a company statement of intended use, not a flight clearance. — [Reuters, 17 September 2026](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/lockheed-martin-reveals-new-secret-missile-fast-tracks-production-2026-09-17/)

### Inferences
- The 2019 Lockheed description of two AIM-120s per bay on the A and C, and the Breaking Defense graphic’s “4 AIM-120” line for the B as well as the A and C, fit a two-per-bay internal air-to-air arrangement on all three variants. Sidekick’s third missile per bay is the item Lockheed excluded from the B. That reading is an inference from those two sources, not a released F-35B load sheet.
- “Newest AMRAAM operational on all F-35 variants” (RTX) plus the November 2023 F-35C AIM-120D3 shot (AFOTEC) plus D3 still awaiting a fielding recommendation in late FY2023 (DOT&E) should stay separated. Test activity, a pending fielding decision, and a manufacturer “operational on all variants” sentence are not the same status, and none of them names the F-35B internal station list for D versus D3.
- AIM-120A/B have a published larger span than C/D (see the dimensions section). No source reviewed here says the B-model is, or is not, cleared in an F-35 bay. The safe simulator statement is that public clearances name AMRAAM and, for Block 3F test language, AIM-120C, while C/D are the variants with the smaller published span.

### Gaps
- No official sheet found that states a cleared maximum of external AIM-120s on the F-35B, or a single “full air-to-air” total that adds underwing AIM-120s to the four internal missiles and two AIM-9Xs. The Breaking Defense external menu stops at two AIM-9Xs plus air-to-ground stores and the gun pod.
- No primary source found that names AIM-120B, AIM-120C-5, AIM-120C-7, or AIM-120C-8 as the specific round cleared on the F-35B, as distinct from the family names AIM-120C, AIM-120D, and AIM-120D3.
- No 2026 source found that Sidekick is operationally cleared on the F-35A or F-35C. Status found: announced and described as under contract, excluded from the B.
- AIM-260 length, diameter, span, mass, guidance class, and range are not published by the government or Lockheed in the sources opened. The magazine’s “120++ miles” and “likely multimode” wording are not official figures and are not repeated here as facts.
- Unclassified DOT&E’s FY2024 F-35 summary says a classified combined IOT&E report on Block 3F was published in February 2024. That unclassified summary does not contain a weapon-by-weapon clearance list. — [DOT&E FY2024 F-35](https://www.dote.osd.mil/Portals/97/pub/reports/FY2024/dod/2024f-35jsf.pdf)

## What is the status of AIM-9X, ASRAAM, and Meteor on the F-35, especially the F-35B?

### Takeaway
AIM-9X was a Block 3F weapon, and separation testing was external-only for every F-35 variant. UK ASRAAM was in Block 3F weapons testing and, by a July 2022 ministerial answer, was cleared on the UK F-35B together with AMRAAM; station and Block 6 service status are less firmly documented. Meteor on the F-35B is internal-bay integration testing, led by the UK, with Italy sponsoring the F-35A, and a June 2025 ministerial answer put in-service capability in the early 2030s.

### Cited Findings
- DOT&E F-35 report on 2017 activity: “Weapons separation testing of the AIM-9X missile (external only for all variants).” F-35C work included a redesigned outboard wing structure “required to support carriage of the AIM-9X air-to-air missile on a pylon,” after excessive loads with the AIM-9X on the outboard external pylons. A Block 3F weapons-delivery event numbered 318 is “AIM-9X BLOCK 2,” software 3FR5.06, December 2016, assessment Successful. Other AIM-9X events in July and August 2016 were also Successful. — [FY2017 DOT&E F-35 report copy](http://www.aereo.jor.br/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/F-35-report-2017.pdf)
- Same report, Block 3F mission-systems testing: remaining Block 3F weapons included the UK Paveway IV and the Advanced Short-Range Air-to-Air Missile (ASRAAM). Weapons-delivery events 315 (UK ASRAAM, software 3FR6.01, February 2017) and 314 (UK ASRAAM, software 3FR6.12, June 2017) were listed as “Analysis in Progress,” not as a completed successful score, at the time of that report. — [FY2017 DOT&E F-35 report copy](http://www.aereo.jor.br/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/F-35-report-2017.pdf)
- U.S. Air Force news article on the close of Block 3F weapons-delivery-accuracy tests (events running to a gun test at the beginning of December; page fetched 22 October 2026): the Integrated Test Force used all three variants and delivered AIM-120s, the AIM-9X, and the United Kingdom’s ASRAAM. The article says that when squadrons receive 3F software they can load those weapons. — [U.S. Air Force WDA article](https://www.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/1400908/f-35-testers-wrap-up-weapons-delivery-accuracy-tests/)
- AFOTEC, 14 November 2023: AIM-9X and AIM-9X-3 were fired from F-35s on software 30R08.051 in the same developmental test series as the first F-35 AIM-120D3 shot. The article does not say AIM-9X-3 was fleet-released, and it does not name the station. — [AFOTEC, 14 November 2023](https://www.afotec.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/3587984/uott-conducts-8-live-fire-missile-tests/)
- NAVAIR, 21 June 2012: inert AIM-9X carried on the F-35B’s starboard pylon during a flight-test load, with the AIM-120 in the weapon bay. — [NAVAIR, 21 June 2012](https://www.navair.navy.mil/node/17256)
- Breaking Defense infographic: external weapon options for A, B, and C each include “2 AIM-9X.” Internal options on that graphic do not list AIM-9X. — [Breaking Defense F-35 infographic PDF](https://cdn2.hubspot.net/hubfs/2097098/F-35_infographic_R8.pdf)

The card does not list Meteor or ASRAAM. — [Lockheed F-35B product card, 2023](https://www.f35.com/content/dam/lockheed-martin/aero/f35/documents/F-35B%20Product%20Card.pdf)
- The War Zone and The Aviationist, 28 February 2025, describing released photos: a Meteor in an F-35B weapon bay beside an AIM-120, with cropped fins. The War Zone’s statement that fighters “will carry a maximum of four Meteors, all internally” is the magazine’s claim; it is not in the RAF, Royal Navy, or MBDA releases quoted above. — [The War Zone, February 2025](https://www.twz.com/air/f-35b-has-flown-with-meteor-long-range-air-to-air-missile); [The Aviationist, 28 February 2025](https://theaviationist.com/2025/02/28/f-35-first-flight-meteor/)

### Inferences
- AIM-9X on the F-35, including the B, is an external-pylon missile for Block 3F. The DOT&E sentence is explicit: external only, all variants. The infographic’s “2 AIM-9X” external line matches a pair of wing stations, and the 2012 F-35B flight put the missile on a wing pylon. Nothing reviewed here clears AIM-9X in the internal bay.
- UK ASRAAM was already in the Block 3F test program in 2017 and was named in a 2022 clearance answer. Block 6 was a later sustainment standard with a 2024 F-35 plan stated in 2021. Those are two layers. A simulator that shows “ASRAAM on a UK F-35B” is supported for the weapon family from 2022. A simulator that specifically shows “Block 6” needs a later in-service confirmation that this pass did not find.
- Meteor’s F-35B path is bay carriage, not a wingtip substitute for ASRAAM. The Royal Navy’s March 2025 wording is weapons-bay loading and an inert launch or release, during integration trials. In-service use on the F-35B remained an early-2030s estimate in June 2025 and, according to the April 2026 press report, was unchanged. Italy’s public role in the releases is sponsorship of the F-35A integration, while Italy also operates the F-35B. No source here gives Italian F-35Bs a separate Meteor clearance ahead of the UK-led B campaign.
- RTX’s claim that AMRAAM is the only radar-guided air-to-air missile cleared on the F-35 is consistent with Meteor still being in test.

### Gaps
- No MoD or JPO sentence found that says ASRAAM is wingtip-only, or that names stations 1 and 11. That station claim rests on the Think Defence compilation.
- No 2024, 2025, or 2026 MoD confirmation found that ASRAAM Block 6, as opposed to ASRAAM in general, has entered service on the F-35B. The 2024 date is a 2021 plan reported by the press.
- No official count of Meteors per F-35B bay or per aircraft. Photo captions describe one Meteor in a bay with an AIM-120 during early captive/inert trials.
- Cropped-fin span for the F-35 store is not printed on the MBDA datasheet. The datasheet only shows that a cropped-fin variant exists.
- No source found that Italy’s F-35B carries ASRAAM. ASRAAM public users in the MBDA page are the UK and Australia, plus an Indian order for Jaguar, not an Italian F-35 fit.
- AIM-9X-3’s relationship to “Block II” or “Block II+” nomenclature is not defined in the AFOTEC article. The article’s label is “AIM-9X-3,” fired in a 2023 developmental test.

## Published mass, length, diameter, and wingspan

### Takeaway
Navy and NAVAIR sheets agree on AIM-120 and AIM-9X length and diameter and disagree on AIM-120D mass by two pounds. They also disagree with an older Air Force sheet and an Air Force graphic caption on AIM-120 mass, range, and C-model span, and the AIM-9X span captions disagree by several inches. ASRAAM and Meteor have official length, mass, and diameter and no published wingspan in the current specification blocks. AIM-260 dimensions are not published.

### Cited Findings
- AIM-120, U.S. Navy fact file, 23 September 2021: length 12 feet; diameter 7 inches; wingspan AIM-120A/B 21 inches, AIM-120C/D 19 inches; weight AIM-120A/B/C-4 348 pounds, AIM-120C-5/6/7/D 356 pounds. Speed: Classified. No range line. — [U.S. Navy AIM-120 fact file](https://www.navy.mil/Resources/Fact-Files/Display-FactFiles/Article/2168352/aim-120-advanced-medium-range-air-to-air-missile-amraam/)
- AIM-120, NAVAIR, fetched 22 October 2026: length 12 feet; diameter 7 inches; wingspan AIM-120A/B 21 inches, AIM-120C/D 19 inches; weight AIM-120A/B/C/C-4 348 pounds, AIM-120C5/6/7 356 pounds, AIM-120D 358 pounds. Speed: Classified. Range: Classified. The D-model mass, 358 pounds, contradicts the Navy fact file’s 356 pounds for C-5/6/7/D. — [NAVAIR AMRAAM](https://www.navair.navy.mil/product/AMRAAM)
- AIM-120, Air Force fact sheet (text returned in search on 22 October 2026; the page still calls the F-35 “developmental” and says the missile is “scheduled to be operational beyond 2000”): length 143.9 inches (366 centimeters); launch weight 335 pounds (150.75 kilograms); diameter 7 inches (17.78 centimeters); wingspan 20.7 inches (52.58 centimeters); range “20+ miles.” This single set of numbers does not distinguish A/B from C/D and conflicts with both current Navy sheets on mass and span. Treat it as a legacy sheet, not as the C/D figure. — [U.S. Air Force AIM-120 fact sheet](https://www.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/104576/aim-120-amraam/)

- AIM-9X, NAVAIR, fetched 22 October 2026: length 9.9 feet (3.02 meters); launch weight 186 pounds (84.37 kg); diameter 5 inches (0.13 meters); wingspan 17.6 inches (0.45 meters). Range: Classified. Wingspan 13.9 inches contradicts NAVAIR’s 17.6 inches. Same ambiguity about which surface is meant. — [Air Force news art captions](https://www.af.mil/News/Art/igtag/missiles/)
- ASRAAM, MBDA product page, fetched 22 October 2026: weight 88 kg; length 2.9 m; diameter 166 mm. No wingspan and no range on this page. — [MBDA ASRAAM](https://www.mbda-systems.com/products/air-dominance/asraam)
- ASRAAM, UK Ministry of Defence infographic (asset still on publishing.service.gov.uk; the graphic says the missile is “being integrated onto the F-35,” so the graphic predates a completed F-35 clearance): weight 88 kg; length 2.9 m; diameter 166 mm; range “In excess of 25 km.” No wingspan. — [UK ASRAAM infographic PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a8016e2ed915d74e33f8617/ASRAAM_infographic.pdf)
- Meteor, MBDA datasheet 2023-08-v03: weight 190 kg; length 3.7 m; diameter 178 mm. No wingspan or fin span in the specification block. Full-fin and cropped-fin variants are pictured only. — [MBDA Meteor datasheet PDF](https://mbdainc.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/2023-METEOR-datasheet.pdf)
- AIM-260: Air & Space Forces Magazine lists dimensions as unknown. No manufacturer fact sheet with length, diameter, span, or mass was found. — [Air & Space Forces Magazine, AIM-260](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/weapons/aim-260-jatm/)
- GAU-22/A gun, not a missile, included because the pod is an external store on the B: General Dynamics Ordnance and Tactical Systems sheet marked 2024.08: four-barrel 25 mm, weight 230 lb (104.3 kg), rate of fire up to 3,300 shots per minute, linkless feed, “F-35A internal gun system” or “F-35B/C missionized gun pods.” Drawing callouts printed on the sheet are 83.2 in (2,113 mm), 15.6 in (396.2 mm), 13.3 in (337.5 mm), 11.1 in (281.6 mm), and 10.2 in (259.6 mm). The extracted text does not label which callout is length, width, or height. The sheet does not give ammunition capacity. — [GD-OTS GAU-22/A, 2024.08](https://www.gdots.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/400001948-GAU-22-A-2024-09-V04.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a mesh and a mass model, the current Navy/NAVAIR tables are the ones to prefer for AIM-120 and AIM-9X: 12 ft by 7 in for AMRAAM, with 21 in span on A/B and 19 in on C/D, and 9.9 ft by 5 in and 17.6 in span for AIM-9X. The two-pound AIM-120D disagreement (356 versus 358 lb) and the 17-versus-19-inch AIM-120C span disagreement should be carried as source conflict, not averaged.
- ASRAAM at 2.9 m by 166 mm and 88 kg, and Meteor at 3.7 m by 178 mm and 190 kg, are stable across the MBDA page or datasheet and the UK ASRAAM infographic. Fin span for both is an open mesh input. Meteor’s cropped-fin variant is the one press photos associate with the F-35B bay, but the span of that crop is not published.
- A 12-foot AIM-120 and a 3.7 m (about 12.1-foot) Meteor are close in length. That is consistent with both being discussed as internal-bay stores, and it does not by itself prove a cleared Meteor loadout.

### Gaps
- ASRAAM wingspan or fin span: not published on the current MBDA specification list or the UK infographic.
- Meteor full-fin and cropped-fin spans: not published in the 2023 datasheet text.
- AIM-260: all physical numbers not published.
- Which AIM-9X and AIM-120 “wingspan” figure is the foreplane versus the tail was not defined by the conflicting captions. Do not pick 13.9 versus 17.6, or 17 versus 19, without a drawing.
- No published minimum or maximum carriage mass for the F-35 pylon and bay stations was collected in this pass, beyond the infographic’s bay totals of 3,500 lb internal and 15,000 lb internal-plus-external for the B.

## Guidance class only

### Takeaway
Published classes are active radar for AIM-120 and Meteor, and infrared for AIM-9X and ASRAAM. AIM-260’s guidance class is not officially published. Midcourse inertial updates are part of the published AIM-120 and Meteor descriptions. No seeker, frequency, or countermeasure detail is recorded here.

### Cited Findings
- AIM-120, NAVAIR specification line: “Guidance System: Active radar.” The narrative also says long-range shots use inertial guidance with updated target information via data link from the launch aircraft, then the missile’s own radar. — [NAVAIR AMRAAM](https://www.navair.navy.mil/product/AMRAAM)
- AIM-120, Navy fact file, 23 September 2021: “an active radar in conjunction with an inertial reference unit.” — [U.S. Navy AIM-120 fact file](https://www.navy.mil/Resources/Fact-Files/Display-FactFiles/Article/2168352/aim-120-advanced-medium-range-air-to-air-missile-amraam/)
- AIM-120, RTX page: “a combination of inertial guidance, midcourse updates, and an on-board active radar.” — [RTX AMRAAM](https://www.rtx.com/raytheon/what-we-do/air/amraam-missile)
- AIM-9X, NAVAIR specification line: “Guidance System: Solid-state, infrared homing system.” The narrative uses the phrase “imaging infrared” for Block II. — [NAVAIR AIM-9X](https://www.navair.navy.mil/product/AIM-9X-Sidewinder)
- DOT&E 2017 F-35 text: “AIM-9X infrared-guided, short-range, air-to-air missile.” — [FY2017 DOT&E F-35 report copy](http://www.aereo.jor.br/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/F-35-report-2017.pdf)
- ASRAAM, UK MoD, 16 August 2016: “advanced heat-seeking weapon” and “sophisticated infra-red seeker.” — [GOV.UK, 16 August 2016](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/mod-signs-184-million-contract-to-secure-air-to-air-missiles-for-the-f-35)
- ASRAAM, MBDA page: does not print a guidance-class label in the specification block. It does say lock-on before launch and lock-on after launch. — [MBDA ASRAAM](https://www.mbda-systems.com/products/air-dominance/asraam)
- Meteor, MBDA datasheet: “Active RF”; navigation and guidance “Inertial mid-course with data link” and “Autonomous terminal guidance.” — [MBDA Meteor datasheet PDF](https://mbdainc.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/2023-METEOR-datasheet.pdf)
- Meteor, Royal Navy, 6 March 2025: “radar-guided.” — [Royal Navy, 6 March 2025](https://www.royalnavy.mod.uk/news/2025/march/06/250306-meteor-missile-trialed-on-f35-jet)
- AIM-260: no official guidance-class label found. Magazine text that says a multimode seeker is “likely” is speculation and is not used here.

### Inferences
- For a simulator’s weapon class flags, AIM-120 and Meteor are active-radar beyond-visual-range missiles with an inertial midcourse phase, and AIM-9X and ASRAAM are infrared short-range missiles. ASRAAM’s official wording in the 2016 MoD release is heat-seeking / infrared. The current MBDA page does not add the word “imaging.”
- Meteor’s ramjet is propulsion, not guidance. The datasheet calls it a “solid fuel variable flow ducted rocket (ramjet).” That is the physical distinction from AMRAAM’s solid-propellant rocket on the Navy fact file. — [MBDA Meteor datasheet PDF](https://mbdainc.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/2023-METEOR-datasheet.pdf); [U.S. Navy AIM-120 fact file](https://www.navy.mil/Resources/Fact-Files/Display-FactFiles/Article/2168352/aim-120-advanced-medium-range-air-to-air-missile-amraam/)

### Gaps
- AIM-260 guidance class: not published.
- Whether the public “imaging infrared” phrase for AIM-9X Block II also applies to the “AIM-9X-3” round fired in the 2023 F-35 test: the AFOTEC article does not say.

## Published range bands and launch constraints

### Takeaway
Current U.S. Navy and NAVAIR sheets classify AIM-120 and AIM-9X range and speed. The only numeric AIM-120 range found on an Air Force fact sheet is a legacy “20+ miles” figure that does not match those sheets. ASRAAM has an official “in excess of 25 km” on an older MoD graphic. Meteor has no range number on the MBDA datasheet; the Royal Navy has published “as great as 120 miles” and up to four times the speed of sound. No unclassified minimum launch speed or altitude was found for any of these missiles.

### Cited Findings
- AIM-120, NAVAIR: “Range: Classified.” “Speed: Classified.” — [NAVAIR AMRAAM](https://www.navair.navy.mil/product/AMRAAM)
- AIM-120, Navy fact file, 23 September 2021: speed classified; no range field. — [U.S. Navy AIM-120 fact file](https://www.navy.mil/Resources/Fact-Files/Display-FactFiles/Article/2168352/aim-120-advanced-medium-range-air-to-air-missile-amraam/)
- AIM-120, legacy Air Force fact sheet: “Range: 20+ miles (17.38+ nautical miles).” The same sheet’s mass and span conflict with the 2021 Navy file, and its prose still treats the F-35 as developmental. Do not use 20+ miles as the published maximum of AIM-120C or AIM-120D. — [U.S. Air Force AIM-120 fact sheet](https://www.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/104576/aim-120-amraam/)
- AIM-9X, NAVAIR: “Range: Classified.” “Speed: Classified.” — [NAVAIR AIM-9X](https://www.navair.navy.mil/product/AIM-9X-Sidewinder)
- AIM-9X, Air Force news-art caption: “Range: More than 10 miles.” This contradicts NAVAIR’s “Classified” and is a caption, not the NAVAIR specification table. — [Air Force news art captions](https://www.af.mil/News/Art/igtag/missiles/)
- ASRAAM, UK MoD infographic: “Range: In excess of 25 km.” The current MBDA product page does not repeat a range number. — [UK ASRAAM infographic PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a8016e2ed915d74e33f8617/ASRAAM_infographic.pdf); [MBDA ASRAAM](https://www.mbda-systems.com/products/air-dominance/asraam)
- Meteor, MBDA datasheet: no kilometer or nautical-mile range. The performance claim on the sheet is qualitative (“largest No-Escape Zone”). — [MBDA Meteor datasheet PDF](https://mbdainc.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/2023-METEOR-datasheet.pdf)
- Meteor, Royal Navy, 6 March 2025: “at up to four times the speed of sound – and at ranges as great as 120 miles from the aircraft which fires it.” The sentence describes Meteor as the UK’s primary air-to-air missile and then says it is currently carried on operations by Typhoon, with F-35 work still in progress. The 120 miles and Mach-4 figures are service-published Meteor performance, not an F-35 launch-envelope table, and they are not on the MBDA datasheet. — [Royal Navy, 6 March 2025](https://www.royalnavy.mod.uk/news/2025/march/06/250306-meteor-missile-trialed-on-f35-jet)
- Aircraft envelope, not a missile minimum: the 2017 DOT&E F-35 text discusses clearing the F-35B Block 3F flight envelope, “Mach 1.6, 630 knots, and 7.0 g.” That is the aircraft envelope under test, not a published minimum launch speed or altitude for AIM-120, AIM-9X, or ASRAAM. — [FY2017 DOT&E F-35 report copy](http://www.aereo.jor.br/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/F-35-report-2017.pdf)
- AIM-260 range: not published by Lockheed or DoD in the sources opened. Reuters, 17 September 2026, says greater range than existing air-to-air missiles and gives no figure. — [Reuters, 17 September 2026](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/lockheed-martin-reveals-new-secret-missile-fast-tracks-production-2026-09-17/)

### Inferences
- A simulator should treat AIM-120 and AIM-9X maximum range as not published under current U.S. Navy sheets. Printing “20+ miles” or “more than 10 miles” would repeat legacy or caption figures that the current specification pages do not support.
- ASRAAM “in excess of 25 km” is the only short-range missile number here that comes from a UK government graphic, with the caveat that the graphic’s integration wording is older than the 2022 clearance answer and the current MBDA page omits range.
- Meteor “120 miles” and “four times the speed of sound” can be shown as a Royal Navy published band dated 6 March 2025, labeled as a service description of the missile rather than an MBDA fact-sheet maximum or an F-35 release limit.

### Gaps
- Unclassified minimum launch speed, minimum altitude, or maximum launch Mach for AIM-120, AIM-9X, ASRAAM, Meteor, and AIM-260 on the F-35: not found.
- AIM-120C versus AIM-120D range comparison: not published as numbers on the Navy, NAVAIR, or RTX pages reviewed. Trade-press percentages were not used.
- AIM-260 range: not published.

## Gun ammunition as an air-to-air store: round type and capacity

### Takeaway
The F-35A carries a GAU-22/A internally. The F-35B and F-35C have no internal gun and can carry the same gun in a centerline missionized pod. The round publicly named in an Air Force gun-test article is the 25 mm PGU-23/U target-practice round. A later press report attributes PGU-48/B as the operational round to a program spokesman. Published capacity is 181 rounds for the F-35A in 2015 full-capacity test reporting, and 220 rounds for the pod in a 2010 engineering briefing. The 2024 gun fact sheet gives rate of fire and gun mass, not round count or a PGU number.

### Cited Findings
- U.S. Air Force, Edwards Air Force Base gun-test article (page fetched 22 October 2026): the GAU-22/A is a four-barrel Gatling gun firing 25 mm rounds, integrated internally on the F-35A. “In the other variants, the gun is mounted to the outside as a pod.” Ground tests used “a target practice round, the PGU-23/U, which fires from the gun but does not explode on impact.” The article text retrieved here does not itself print the magazine capacity. — [U.S. Air Force Edwards gun article](https://www.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/610801/ground-testing-for-f-35-gun-conducted-at-edwards-afb/)
- USNI News, 20 August 2015: video of 181 rounds fired through the GAU-22/A of an F-35A on 14 August 2015. — [USNI News, 20 August 2015](https://news.usni.org/2015/08/20/video-181-rounds-fired-from-f-35a-lighting-ii-joint-strike-fighter-gatling-gun)
- The Aviationist, 21 August 2015, citing the Integrated Test Force / Lockheed description of that test: AF-2 fired a “full capacity of 181 rounds” of PGU-23/U practice ammunition. The same article says B and C carry the gun in an external pod “capable to hold 220 rounds.” The 220 figure in that article is not footnoted to a document. — [The Aviationist, 21 August 2015](https://theaviationist.com/2015/08/21/video-f-35-25mm-gun-full-capacity/)
- U.S. Air Force, Luke Air Force Base, article dated from an 20 October 2020 photo caption: a bulk loader “houses four magazines, while each magazine holds 181 rounds of ammunition,” and the loader “loads 25 mm rounds into the aircraft’s GAU-22 25mm cannon’s linkless ammunition handling system.” The caption’s 181 is the loader magazine, which matches the 2015 “full capacity” firing count. It also says the loader holds 724 rounds (four magazines). That 724 is loader capacity, not aircraft capacity. — [U.S. Air Force, Luke AFB](https://www.aetc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/2417494/armament-shop-maintains-f-35-weapons-system-equipment/)
- NDIA briefing, 2010, “F-35 Lightning II Missionized Gun System”: pod ammunition handling system “220-rnd”; gun “3000 spm” in that briefing; “1017 lb fully loaded”; “27 in wide, 32 in high, 146 in long.” The 3,000 shots-per-minute figure in this 2010 briefing conflicts with the manufacturer’s 2024 sheet of up to 3,300. The 2024 sheet does not restate 220 rounds or the pod envelope. — [NDIA 2010 Missionized Gun System PDF](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2010/armament/TuesdayLandmarkADouglasParker.pdf); [GD-OTS GAU-22/A, 2024.08](https://www.gdots.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/400001948-GAU-22-A-2024-09-V04.pdf)
- GD-OTS 2024.08: muzzle velocity is printed separately for “TP, HEI ammunition” at 3,560 ft/s (1,085 m/s) and for “API ammunition” at 3,400 ft/s (1,036 m/s). The sheet names those ammunition classes and does not give PGU stock numbers. — [GD-OTS GAU-22/A, 2024.08](https://www.gdots.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/400001948-GAU-22-A-2024-09-V04.pdf)
- Breaking Defense infographic: F-35A internal options include “1 GAU-22 25mm cannon.” F-35B and F-35C external options include “1 GAU-22 25mm cannon.” The F-35B internal column does not include a gun. — [Breaking Defense F-35 infographic PDF](https://cdn2.hubspot.net/hubfs/2097098/F-35_infographic_R8.pdf)
- NAVAIR, 21 June 2012: F-35B BF-2 flew with a “centerline 25 mm gun pod.” — [NAVAIR, 21 June 2012](https://www.navair.navy.mil/node/17256)
- The War Zone, reporting a Joint Program Office spokesperson (article fetched via search 22 October 2026; the underlying budget PDF was not opened): the operational round for the GAU-22/A is the PGU-48/B frangible armor-piercing round, and the F-35A magazine is described there as 180 rounds, with a GPU-9/A pod of 220 rounds on the B and C. The 180-round figure conflicts with the 2015 full-capacity reporting of 181 and with the 2020 loader caption of 181. The pod designation GPU-9/A was not confirmed on the GD-OTS sheet, which says “missionized gun pods.” — [The War Zone on the F-35A gun](https://www.twz.com/air/f-35as-beleaguered-25mm-cannon-is-finally-effective)

### Inferences
- For the F-35B, the gun is an optional centerline external store, not an internal weapon. When it is fitted it occupies the centerline, as in the 2012 test photo that also carried bay weapons and a wing AIM-9X. Round count to use, if a single public engineering number is required, is 220 from the 2010 missionized-gun briefing, with the caveat that the 2024 gun sheet does not repeat it.
- For the F-35A, 181 rounds is the number tied to a named “full capacity” firing and to the Air Force loader caption. A later press account of 180 rounds should stay visible as a conflict, not be silently preferred.
- PGU-23/U is the practice round documented by the Air Force for early gun tests. PGU-48/B is reported by the trade press as what a JPO spokesperson called the current operational round. Both can be named, with that difference in source quality. The manufacturer sheet supports that TP, HEI, and API classes exist for the gun and does not identify which PGU the services issue.

### Gaps
- An opened DoD budget exhibit or Rheinmetall data sheet naming PGU-48/B was not retrieved. The PGU-48/B identification here is second-hand via The War Zone.
- Current, post-2010 official pod capacity and pod designation (missionized gun pod versus GPU-9/A) were not confirmed on a 2024–2026 government fact sheet.
- Mass of a single 25 mm round, and any air-to-air-specific mix of TP, HEI, and API, were not published in the sources opened. Terminal effects were not researched.
