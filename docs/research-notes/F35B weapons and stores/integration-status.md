# F-35B weapons integration status (public sources, as of 2026-10-22)

Scope is what a civilian simulator can treat as cleared on an operational F-35B (US Marine Corps, UK, Italy) versus what has only been flown in test or listed on a roadmap. Sources are public. Employment envelopes, nuclear-store details, and electronic-attack internals are not reconstructed. Where two sources disagree, both are cited and the later one is preferred only when it is at least as primary.

A status table for a default loadout versus a backlog is in the first question. It is an evidence grade, not a classified clearance matrix. DOT&E’s combined Block 3F IOT&E report (February 2024) is classified and is not used here; only the unclassified annual-report text is.

## Which weapons does a current operational F-35B (US Marine Corps, UK, Italy) have cleared, as opposed to demonstrated in a test flight or listed on a roadmap?

### Takeaway

As of the latest official US report (DOT&E FY2025, submitted 13 March 2026, covering events through 30 September 2025), no new F-35 combat capability had been fielded in FY2025, and no combat-capable TR-3 jet had been delivered. The weapons an operational F-35B can be shown from public sources to employ are the Block 2B set (AIM-120, GBU-12, GBU-32) plus the Block 3F additions that finished developmental weapons-delivery testing (AIM-9X and the podded gun), and, for the UK only, ASRAAM and Paveway IV as stated by the Ministry of Defence on 12 July 2022. Meteor, SPEAR 3, JSM, StormBreaker, AARGM-ER, and LRASM are not in that cleared set.

### Cited Findings

Status grades used below:

- **Cleared (public claim):** a service or ministry has said the jet may employ it, or operational crews have expended it as part of an IOC declaration.
- **SDD-tested:** dropped or fired in the Block 3F weapons-delivery campaign. Not, by itself, a 2024–2026 fleet-employment report.
- **Test only:** captive carry, fit check, or a developmental/operational test event after Block 3F.
- **Roadmap:** named in a plan, sale, or ministerial target, with no public clearance.

### Inferences

- For a default operational F-35B in 2026, use a TR-2 / Block 3F jet, not a combat-capable TR-3 jet. The public record through 30 September 2025 says the delivered TR-3 aircraft were accepted with combat capability turned off.
- USMC stealth default that stays inside cleared public stores: two AIM-120 internally and two GBU-32 or two GBU-12 internally. Add two AIM-9X on the wingtips only when external missiles are wanted. Leave the gun pod off that stealth load; it is a centerline external store.
- UK stealth default: two AMRAAM and two Paveway IV, plus ASRAAM as a cleared missile whose station the 2022 answer does not specify. Do not load Meteor or SPEAR 3.
- Italian default should not exceed AIM-120 and GBU-12 unless a later primary source is added. The August 2024 SEAD mission statement does not identify an anti-radiation missile. AARGM-ER was still an integration item for the USMC in February 2025.
- SDB I, JSOW, StormBreaker, GBU-54, LRASM, AARGM-ER, Meteor, SPEAR 3, and JSM belong in a backlog, not in the jet the pilot “has today.”

### Gaps

- No public 2024–2026 USMC, RAF, or Italian document was found that reprints the full cleared-store list station by station. The 12 July 2022 UK answer is the last primary clearance sentence retrieved for the UK, and it is four years old.
- The parliamentary page itself returned HTTP 403 on fetch, so the 12 July 2022 quotation depends on the search extract.
- FY2022 DOT&E’s F-35 chapter was not separately extracted. FY2024 and FY2025 supersede it for “what is fielded now,” but they do not restate the Block 3F store list.
- No CRS report that names weapon milestones was retrieved.
- No primary Israeli or IAF list of F-35I-unique stores was retrieved, so Israel is a gap rather than a cleared-weapon row. F-35I is an A-variant airframe, not a B.
- Whether UK ASRAAM and Paveway IV are cleared for internal bays, external pylons, or both is not stated in the 2022 answer.
- Nothing retrieved confirms that the Marine Corps met the FY2025 StormBreaker EOC or the FY2026 LRASM EOC. The latest DOT&E cut-off used here is 30 September 2025. October 2025 through 22 October 2026 is not covered by a later DOT&E or JPO weapons release in this pass.
- The Italian Navy IOC claim of a SEAD mission has no public store attached to it in the sources read.

## What did Block 3F declare for weapons, and what has slipped or been added under Block 4 and TR-3 through 2026?

### Takeaway

Block 3F’s developmental weapons plan was GBU-12, GBU-31/32, JSOW-C1, SDB I, AIM-120C, AIM-9X, and the gun, plus UK ASRAAM and Paveway IV in the test campaign. Block 4 was supposed to add weapons and other capability on top of that, enabled by TR-3. By 3 September 2025 GAO reported the reduced Block 4 subprogram slipping to 2031 at the earliest, and by 30 September 2025 DOT&E still had no combat-capable TR-3 delivery and no new combat capability fielded in that fiscal year.

### Cited Findings

- A DOT&E F-35 assessment preserved in an Australian Parliament document store, written when Block 3F flight test was still planned to finish in 2017, says the Block 3F weapons-delivery-accuracy plan “will test Block 3F capabilities to employ the GBU-12 Paveway II laser-guided bomb, GBU-31/32 Joint Direct Attack Munition (JDAM), Navy Joint Stand-off Weapon (JSOW)-C1, Small Diameter Bomb I (SDB-1), AIM-120C Advanced Medium-Range Air-to-Air Missile (AMRAAM), AIM-9X, and the gun in the full operating environment of each variant.” The approved plan then had 48 TEMP events plus two deferred from Block 2B. The program office had cut the near-term list to 10 events for the F-35A and 5 for the F-35B and F-35C. This is a plan, not a completion report, and “each variant” does not override the later public fact that the B bay is a 1,000 lb bay. — [Australian Parliament document store copy of the DOT&E assessment](https://www.aph.gov.au/DocumentStore.ashx?id=7dc1ba4b-4efb-4354-b25f-d26a1d25dfd5)
- An Air Force news article on the end of that campaign says weapons-delivery-accuracy flights began in July 2013 and used all three variants. Stores released in the campaign included AIM-120, AIM-9X, the UK ASRAAM, Paveway IV, GBU-39, GBU-12, GBU-31, and AGM-154 JSOW, followed by GAU-22 gun events (internal on the A, pod on the B and C). The article says the fleet, once it received production 3F software, could load those weapons. The extracted text has no dateline and does not break results out by variant. It says 55 weapons were delivered excluding the gun. — [US Air Force](https://www.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/1400908/f-35-testers-wrap-up-weapons-delivery-accuracy-tests/)
- DOT&E FY2021 records the development stack: Block 2B on TR-1 (Marine Corps IOC), Block 3i on TR-2 (Air Force IOC), then Block 3F. It says the final planned Block 3F software was 3FR6, and that later 30-series software on Block 3F hardware was being used both to fix deficiencies and to add capability. — [DOT&E FY2021 F-35](https://www.dote.osd.mil/Portals/97/pub/reports/FY2021/dod/2021f35jsf.pdf)
- DOT&E FY2024 (report text covering that fiscal year): IOT&E was completed in September 2023. A classified combined IOT&E and live-fire report on Block 3F, with an annex on Block 4 testing to date, was published in February 2024. Full-rate production was approved in March 2024. The acquisition decision directed separate major subprograms for engine modernization and for Block 4. TR-3 was the planned host for 30R07-class capability rehosted as 40R01, with 30R08-class capability to follow as 40R02. Lot 15 TR-3 deliveries planned for July 2023 were refused until July 2024. The services then accepted TR-3 aircraft with truncated software. By the end of FY2024, 41 TR-3 aircraft had been delivered. “No combat-capable TR-3 aircraft have been delivered to the U.S. Services to date” appears in that report. In February 2024 the operational test team stopped 30R08 testing for Category I deficiencies and software stability, after some AIM-120 and AIM-9X events and four close-air-support events. — [DOT&E FY2024 F-35](https://www.dote.osd.mil/Portals/97/pub/reports/FY2024/dod/2024f-35jsf.pdf)
- DOT&E FY2025 adds that 30R06 and 30R08 took longer than planned, and that 30R07 and 30R08 delivered or would deliver with less than the capability originally planned for them. The program had not decided whether there would be another 30-series build after 30R08. TR-3 operational test was being replanned to start at 40R03 because 40R01 and 40R02 were not suitable. Funding constraints “will make it difficult to plan for and accomplish adequate weapons testing with 40R03.” Block 4 TEMP Increment 3 annexes, approved November 2024, cover 40R02, 40R03, and 41R01, including “new Electromagnetic Warfare capability enabled by upgraded hardware on TR-3 aircraft starting in production lot 19.” That is a test-plan statement, not a fielding statement. — [DOT&E FY2025 Annual Report](https://www.dote.osd.mil/Portals/97/pub/reports/FY2025/Other/2025Annual-Report.pdf)
- GAO-25-107632, published 3 September 2025: Block 4 began as a 66-capability effort the program said in 2018 it would deliver by 2026. Cost had already grown from $10.6 billion to $16.5 billion as of the 2021 estimate. In May 2024 GAO had reported a completion date of 2029. The rebaselined, smaller Block 4 major subprogram was, by this report, expected no earlier than 2031, limited to capabilities that do not need the engine and power-thermal upgrade. A 2024 feasibility review had found many original Block 4 capabilities would not reach the fleet until the mid-2030s. Electronic warfare, weapons, communication, and navigation were among the categories officials said would remain inside the reduced subprogram. As of May 2025 the program office could not give GAO the approved capability list. Lockheed Martin, according to program officials, planned to begin delivering combat-capable TR-3 aircraft in 2026, about three years late. As of July 2025 the integrated core processor was no longer the driver; the Next Generation Distributed Aperture System was estimated not to finish until 2026; TR-3 software was still unstable in ground and flight test, including radar and cockpit-display problems. — [GAO-25-107632, 3 September 2025](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-25-107632/index.html)
- UK Defence Journal, 21 April 2026, still described Block 4 completion as “around 2029” and the cost as about $16.5 billion. That matches the pre-rebaseline figures, not GAO’s 3 September 2025 date of 2031 for a smaller Block 4. Prefer GAO for the US program date. — [UK Defence Journal, 21 April 2026](https://ukdefencejournal.org.uk/meteor-f-35-integration-shows-no-signs-of-urgency/); later and more specific — [GAO, 3 September 2025](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-25-107632/index.html)
- An unclassified Naval Postgraduate School symposium paper by the F-35 partner-weapons lead says the enterprise was averaging more than 90 months from a formal weapons requirement to fleet release, and that seven years was the working estimate. It is a process critique, not a store-by-store status list. The file is associated with a 2026 symposium identifier (SYM-AM-26-142); the paper itself is not a JPO release. — [NPS acquisition symposium paper](https://www.dair.nps.edu/bitstream/123456789/5586/1/SYM-AM-26-142.pdf)

### Inferences

- Block 3F is the last publicly completed weapons baseline for a fleet F-35B. Block 4 has added test events and plans, not a new fielded combat load, through at least 30 September 2025.
- “Slipped” is well supported for the Block 4/TR-3 schedule (2026, then 2029, then a smaller set in 2031) and for UK Meteor and SPEAR 3 (mid-2020s language, then 2027, then early 2030s, with a June 2026 secondary report of an FY2028–29 SPEAR target).
- “Added” under Block 4, in public plans rather than fleet clearance, includes SDB II, AARGM-ER, external AGM-158-series missiles, JSM on the A/C, Meteor, SPEAR 3, Sidekick on the C, and new EW tied to lot-19 TR-3 hardware. GBU-54 appears as a 30R08 test event, which may be a Block 4 weapon on TR-2, but DOT&E does not say it was fielded.
- A sim backlog should be gated on TR-3 combat software, which public sources still treated as undelivered for combat use. Putting Block 4 weapons on a Block 3F jet overstates what the airplane can fire.

### Gaps

- The February 2024 DOT&E IOT&E report that would be the authoritative Block 3F effectiveness statement is classified. Unclassified annual reports explicitly omit those effectiveness results.
- GAO said that as of May 2025 officials would not list which of the original 66 Block 4 capabilities survived the cut. No public list dated after the 3 September 2025 report was found.
- No source in this pass confirms that combat-capable TR-3 aircraft were or were not delivered between 1 October 2025 and 22 October 2026. GAO’s “2026” date was a plan, not a completion.
- The Air Force WDA wrap-up article’s publication date was not in the extracted text.
- It is not public, in the sources read, which Block 3F stores were later restricted or removed by deficiency reports. DOT&E discusses deficiencies without naming a store-by-store revocation.

## Partner weapons: AIM-132 ASRAAM, Meteor, Paveway IV, SPEAR 3, JSM. Which aircraft (F-35B vs A) and what public milestone?

### Takeaway

ASRAAM and Paveway IV are the only two partner stores the UK government has said a current F-35B is cleared to employ. Meteor has flown inert on an F-35B test jet and is not in service; the UK leads the B and Italy sponsors the A. SPEAR 3 has had one public captive-carry flight on an F-35B with four inert rounds and has not been jettisoned in the releases that describe that flight. JSM is an F-35A/C internal weapon; Norway and Japan have missiles and do not yet have aircraft software that lets them fire them.

## Are there public standard combat loadouts (stealth internal, external beast mode) with named stores and counts for the B?

### Takeaway

There is no Marine Corps, RAF, or Italian “standard loadout” card in the sources reviewed that says “this is the combat load” with station counts. What is public is the bay limit, the payload number, and repeating illustrations: stealth means internal only (two AIM-120 plus two 1,000 lb-class bombs on the B), and a permissive external load adds wingtip missiles, wing bombs, and optionally the gun pod, inside a 15,000 lb weapons payload.

### Inferences

- “Beast mode” is a publicity phrase. No service document in this pass uses it as a named, counted B loadout. For the sim, treat “stealth” as bays only and “permissive” as bays plus the six external hardpoints and the centerline, and do not invent a seventh pylon.

## What public limits should a civilian sim respect?

### Takeaway

Model the categories that services name in public: two internal bays with a 1,000 lb-class air-to-ground store on the B, external pylons, a centerline gun pod, and an onboard electronic-warfare and electro-optical suite. Do not model a nuclear store on the B, do not model an external electronic-attack pod as if it were a cleared USMC/UK/Italy store, and do not fill in envelopes, bay-door radar effects, or how the EW system works.

### Cited Findings

- Nuclear: an F-35 JPO spokesman, Russ Goemaere, told Breaking Defense that certain F-35As were operationally certified on 12 October 2023 to carry the B61-12, and that the certification does not extend to the F-35B or F-35C. The spokesman said Block 4 is not required for that F-35A capability. This note records only the variant limit and the date. — [Breaking Defense, March 2024](https://breakingdefense.com/2024/03/exclusive-f-35a-officially-certified-to-carry-nuclear-bomb/)
- An earlier public step, not the operational certification, was an F-35A full-weapon-system demonstration that released B61-12 joint test assemblies at Tonopah. The Air Force Nuclear Weapons Center article says that event completed on-aircraft testing for the design-certification phase and that no date had then been released for full operational certification. It does not involve the B. — [Air Force Nuclear Weapons Center](https://www.afnwc.af.mil/News/Article/2799444/f-35a-completes-milestone-5th-gen-fighter-test-with-refurbished-b61-12-nuclear/)
- UK Public Accounts Committee, 31 October 2025: “Only the F-35A variant can carry nuclear weapons.” The UK’s announced nuclear role is for 12 F-35As, not for the F-35B fleet. — [PAC, 31 October 2025](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm5901/cmselect/cmpubacc/1232/report.html)
- Gun pod: publicly described as existing, external, centerline, variant-specific (B pod reported as GPU-9/A, C pod as GPU-8/A), with a GAU-22/A. The A model’s gun is internal. No ballistic envelope, accuracy number, or dispersion figure is repeated here. DOT&E has criticized the internal A-model gun in unclassified reporting over the years; those figures are not copied into this note and are not a B-pod envelope. — [The War Zone, quoting a February 2024 DOT&E passage](https://www.twz.com/air/f-35b-and-f-35c-use-different-25mm-gun-pods-unique-to-each-variant); architecture — [NDIA 2015 brief](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2015/PSAR/RADMMahr.pdf)
- Onboard sensors and EW, named only: the RAF factsheet lists AN/APG-81, an IR search-and-track function, a “full countermeasure suite,” AN/AAQ-37 Distributed Aperture System, an electro-optical targeting system, Link 16, and MADL, and says the pilot can employ “electronic attack capability.” It does not describe techniques, frequencies, or a pod. — [RAF factsheet](https://www.raf.mod.uk/aircraft/current-aircraft/lightning-f35b/)
- DOT&E FY2025 says new electromagnetic-warfare capability tied to upgraded hardware is in the Block 4 test plan for TR-3 aircraft from production lot 19, inside software builds that were not suitable for operational test. That is an existence statement for a future software/hardware increment, not a description of the system and not a fielded pod. — [DOT&E FY2025](https://www.dote.osd.mil/Portals/97/pub/reports/FY2025/Other/2025Annual-Report.pdf)
- DOT&E’s unclassified reports discuss dynamic radar-cross-section measurements as a test requirement and say additional measurements had not been done. They do not publish the measurements. A sim should not invent an RCS change when doors open, beyond the qualitative public point that open doors are the moment of release. — [DOT&E FY2024](https://www.dote.osd.mil/Portals/97/pub/reports/FY2024/dod/2024f-35jsf.pdf); [DOT&E FY2025](https://www.dote.osd.mil/Portals/97/pub/reports/FY2025/Other/2025Annual-Report.pdf)
- Categories that are publicly acknowledged and are enough for a sim store list, without internals: internal bays; external wing pylons; wingtip air-to-air missiles; centerline gun pod on the B; conventional bombs and missiles named in the cleared table; inert test shapes and environmental-data rounds (used for Meteor and SPEAR 3); an internal electro-optical and countermeasures suite. A nuclear store is acknowledged only for the F-35A.

### Inferences

- The sim’s hard limits, based on what is public: no B61 or other nuclear store on any F-35B loadout; no numbers for release envelopes, fuse settings, or lethality; no EW technique, frequency, or pod schematic; no claim that an external jamming pod is a standard USMC, UK, or Italian F-35B store.
- The gun pod can appear as a selectable external object with a published round count of 220 only if that count is attributed to the War Zone / contracting-notice account, not to a flight-manual envelope. Prefer a simple “gun pod fitted / not fitted” if the spec wants to stay closer to government text.
- “Electronic attack” on a current UK or USMC F-35B should be a named aircraft capability, not a store the pilot hangs on a pylon.

### Gaps

- No government source retrieved in this pass confirms an external electronic-attack pod as a fielded, named store on the F-35B. If one exists in a classified or unreleased program, it is outside this note.
- Israeli F-35I modifications are publicly discussed in general terms and were not documented here with a primary store list. Do not add Israeli pods or weapons from memory.
- Unclassified DOT&E gun-accuracy criticism applies in the public literature mainly to the internal F-35A gun. It was not re-quoted here and should not be turned into a B-pod error model without a separate source.

## Any public note on simultaneous release, ripple, or bay-door timing that is unclassified?

### Takeaway

No unclassified official timing for combat bay-door open/close, simultaneous release, or ripple interval was found. What is published is mechanical and procedural: door angles in a Lockheed design article, permission to open the doors in an airshow profile with no duration, and early separation tests of one weapon at a time or of pit drops whose timing is not given.

### Cited Findings

- Code One Magazine, a Lockheed Martin publication, says the inboard weapon-bay doors open ninety-seven degrees to launch AMRAAMs, and open to about forty-five degrees in vertical landing to form side walls for the lift-fan fountain, with a stated ten-percent vertical-lift benefit. It also says F-35A/C bays are slightly larger than the F-35B bay. This is a design description, not a combat timeline, and it does not say how long the doors take to move. — [Code One Magazine](https://www.codeonemagazine.com/article.html?item_id=28)
- The FAA F-35B airshow profile allows a “weapon bay door option” and says doors may be opened during a specified pass and a spiraling descent. It gives speeds and altitudes for the maneuver and no open or close duration. — [FAA F-35B airshow flight profile](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/about/initiatives/airshow/F-35B_Airshow_Flight_Profile.pdf)
- A Headquarters Marine Corps release says F-35B BF-3, flown by Lockheed Martin test pilot Dan Levin, made the first airborne weapons separation of any F-35 variant on 8 August, releasing one inert 1,000 lb GBU-32 from an internal bay at 400 knots and 4,200 feet. The extracted text does not include the year. It describes a single store, not a ripple or a dual-bay release. The marines.mil copy of this article returned “Access Denied” on a direct fetch; the wording is from the search extract of the HQMC page. — [HQMC release](https://www.hqmc.marines.mil/News/Article/Article/553121/f-35-completes-first-airborne-weapons-separation/)
- NAVAIR describes a March pit-drop series on BF-3 that ended with a 500 lb bomb ejection and covered nine internal load combinations, including inert GBU-12, GBU-32, and AIM-120, from the left and right bays. A test pilot said the launches were barely noticeable from the cockpit. No time between releases, no door-cycle time, and no statement that two weapons left simultaneously. — [NAVAIR](https://www.navair.navy.mil/node/18946)
- A Stack Exchange thread guesses “about 3–4 seconds” from watching a video of a Norwegian F-35 launch. The accepted answer treats that guess as a premise. It is not an official timing and should not be coded as one. — [Aviation Stack Exchange](https://aviation.stackexchange.com/questions/60935/how-fast-do-the-f-35-weapons-bay-doors-open-close-during-combat-ops)
- DOT&E annual reports discuss weapon events as captive carry or live fire. They do not publish door timing, ripple spacing, or how many milliseconds the doors stay open. — [DOT&E FY2024 F-35](https://www.dote.osd.mil/Portals/97/pub/reports/FY2024/dod/2024f-35jsf.pdf); [DOT&E FY2025](https://www.dote.osd.mil/Portals/97/pub/reports/FY2025/Other/2025Annual-Report.pdf)

### Inferences

- A sim can show two bays, doors that open before a release and close after it, a wider angle for missile launch than the angle used in a hover, and one store leaving per trigger pull unless a specific store’s own public data says otherwise. SDB and SPEAR are designed to be carried in multiples; that does not publish the interval between them.
- Do not code a 3–4 second door cycle, a simultaneous dual-bay release, or a ripple cadence. Those values were not found in an unclassified official source.
- The 400 knot / 4,200 foot condition is one early test point for one inert bomb. It is not an employment envelope and should not become the only condition under which the sim allows a release.

### Gaps

- Unclassified door-cycle time, ripple interval, and simultaneous-release rules were not published in any JPO, DOT&E, service, or manufacturer source retrieved for this note. The honest spec line is “not publicly released.”
- The year of the 8 August first separation was not in the extracted HQMC text, so it is not stated here.
- Whether later operational software releases one weapon, a pair, or a programmed sequence from an SDB rack is not in the unclassified reports read for this pass.
